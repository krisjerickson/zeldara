# 30 — Soul-lines and the Last Saga (draft for review)

*Oct 19 (round 67). Kris's premise: a story that runs through the whole game and resolves it — Solmyrk and Surtharn broke the soul-cycle, the twins came from the lake, every freed spirit traces **soul-lines** into the land that slowly form a Norse tattoo over the whole map, and after Solmyrk the real gods are freed, lead the spirits home and remake the land. Nothing is built yet: this doc holds the proposals, the conflicts with what exists, the speed plan and the questions. The story is recorded for good (in §9) once the answers are in.*

## 1. The premise (Kris, Oct 19 — as given)

1. Before the quests, the false god **Solmyrk** and **Surtharn** worked together to take over the world.
2. Solmyrk stopped souls going to heaven. The soul-cycle stopped: no children were born, or they were stillborn.
3. Surtharn trapped those souls and put them to work mining the worlds for treasure and gems.
4. Surtharn took the other three quadrants one by one and raised the magma, rock and river barriers.
5. The villagers' sagas remember travelling to the other regions; then each suffered a calamity, its people perished and the barrier rose.
6. This repeated until only the Grasslands were left.
7. Lately, when people of the last village die, their souls appear in the Grasslands bound by mini-bosses.
8. The villagers were losing hope.
9. After 20 years without children, the night after a terrible storm that struck lightning onto runestones all over the Grasslands, two babes drifted to the harbour from the lake in a beautiful, magical floating basket.
10. Their eyes glowed bright blue when the townsfolk took them in; once nursed on goat's milk the glow faded.
11. They are **Zeldara** and **Zeldor**.
12. They brought hope back; the whole town raised and taught them.
13–15. Their gifts showed: they could read old runic texts no one else could, and gems and runestones lit up for them.
16–17. Come of age, the village sent them out to face the doom closing in — knowing only training, perseverance and the world's trials would make them able. (So they still buy their gear — and their spending brings wealth back to the village, which grows with each new quadrant.)
18. **Soul-lines.** Every bound spirit freed traces lines of the runes' light blue into the ground around it and its rune. Close up they are interesting lines; on the world map they slowly form a Norse tattoo across the whole land. They show only once that spirit is freed — so the gaps show which spirits are still bound. *This is the main new mechanic and look for the main game.*
19–23. After Solmyrk: the trapped real god and goddess (**Odin** and **Freya**) must be freed. The citadel crumbles; the feast hall's doors open. Inside, two great magic orbs float joined like an infinity sign; sounds come from inside and the Lodestar trembles. Cast Godsbane into the centre: they burst into millions of shards of light and Odin and Freya appear.
24. They need sprite sheets.
25–26. They tell you to set the Lodestar at Asgard's heart; the new Bifröst rises and they lead the spirits to heaven and Valhalla. Heaven is restored and the cycle rebalanced — new souls for new babies.
27. When they have gone, and only once you are back at the village centre: Odin casts brilliant blue lightning from the sky, and Freya follows with brilliant green vine-like magic that touches down at the village and spreads out over the land.
28. The soul-lines turn into green grass and paths reaching through the whole map (safe passage everywhere); all monsters go; the last mini-volcanoes fall into the sea; Asgard sinks into the lake (a few buildings above the water, no land, no Bifröst); villagers walk out along the paths and houses spring up in every quadrant; a cut-scene celebrates the twins, time passes, children are born again.
29. You can walk anywhere unhurt; little villages with houses, halls, gardens and families with children grow up.
30. Peace, balance, spirits and souls restored — the cycle of life begins again.

## 2. Soul-lines — how to tattoo the land

**What we have to work with.** The island is a circle (radius 265 tiles) with the village at the exact centre and the four realms as quarters (NE Grasslands, SE Wetlands, SW Highlands, NW Ashlands). Each realm has 10 zones; each zone has a runestone and a bindstone next to it (40 in all). Seen whole, the island is already a round medallion — exactly the shape of the inspiration pieces (a round knot frame with a tree, serpents and runes inside).

**The idea.** One *master design* — an original Norse knot drawing — is laid over the whole island once, fitted to its circle and passing through all 40 runestones. It is cut into 40 pieces: each piece is the part of the drawing nearest that zone's runestone. Freeing a spirit lights its piece. Pieces from neighbouring zones meet end to end, so the knot closes up as you go. The Runestone Green's world-tree circle sits at the very centre of the drawing (the "eye" of the tattoo).

**Design candidates for the Lab** (all original; Kris's pictures are style only, described in words, never copied — and no valknut, per the standing rule):

| | Design | What the whole map shows at 40/40 |
|---|---|---|
| A | **The World-Tree** | The trunk rises from the village circle; four great boughs reach into the four realms and split into knotted branches through each zone; roots knot round the coast as a ring. |
| B | **The World-Serpent** | A serpent ring of knotwork runs round the whole coast, biting its tail; four loops dive inward through each realm and interlace at the village circle (like a triquetra-of-four). |
| C | **The Four Beasts** | Four interlaced creatures, one per realm — a stag (Grasslands), a serpent (Wetlands), an eagle (Highlands), a wyrm (Ashlands) — their knotted bodies weaving into the centre, heads at the coast. |
| D | **The Rune Wheel** | A great rune ring round the coast and eight knotted spokes from the centre, each spoke a band of runes; knot rosettes at every runestone. |

**The line itself (close up, in the world)** — also Lab options:

| | Style | In the world |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Knot band** | a double line of rune-blue light, ~2 tiles wide, with true over-under gaps where it crosses itself |
| 2 | **Rune vein** | one bright line with small runes stamped along it, like the rune border on the inspiration ring |
| 3 | **Mote stitch** | a dotted line of light motes that drift slowly along it |
| 4 | **Carved groove** | a groove cut into the ground with light glowing inside it |

**Freeing a spirit** (proposal): the spirit rises; light runs out from the bindstone along its piece, to its runestone and on to the ends of the piece (3–4 s; the camera pulls back a little so you see the shape). The map gets the piece too. The Tome's map page can show the whole tattoo with the unlit pieces as gaps — the "who is still bound" check Kris asked for.

**After the gods** (§1, 28): the same lines become ~3-tile paths of grass and stone, so the whole tattoo is still visible from the map in green — the land wears the tattoo for good.

**The settlements in the rings** (Kris, round 68: "the circles around the runestone are great, this can be outlines for what eventually will turn into the mini settlements at the very end of the game"): each runestone's ring in the World-Tree is the outline of a hamlet. After the credits, free roam opens with all 40 standing — each built round its freed spirit's trade (a smith's ring has a forge, a weaver's has looms, a fisher's has drying racks and a jetty when near water), lived in by villagers who have spread out across Runeheim, the children among them. The ring itself stays as the hamlet's path. Art: one small set of craft buildings per trade (to estimate in round C/D).

## 3. Letting the story unfold (proposal)

**Rule: everyone knows a piece, no one knows the whole.** The player assembles it; the last pieces come from the gods.

| Who tells | What they know | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Village elders** (start) | the stillborn years, the storm, the basket, the glowing eyes — but not where the twins came from | the opening cut-scene (2–3 painted panels) + tavern talk |
| **Freed spirits** (40) | each one memory of its own life and its realm's calamity — out of order, sometimes contradicting the villagers' sagas | one line each when freed, kept in the Tome |
| **Runic tablets** in adventure sites | old prophecy only the twins can read (gift 14) — the gods' side, in riddles | a short panel when read |
| **The freed builders** (Bram, Mira, Dunn, Vela) | how the barriers rose | when freed |
| **Fairy monarchs and familiars' fairies** | memories of Asgard before Solmyrk; the gods' names never said aloud | when befriended |
| **Surtharn and Solmyrk** | their own boasts — half-truths | during the fights |
| **Odin and Freya** | the whole truth, including who the twins are | the end |

- **The Saga page** (Tome): a tapestry of panels in chapters (the cycle, each realm's fall, the binding, the foundlings, the forges, Asgard). Found pieces show their painting or line; missing ones are dark silhouettes. It matches the soul-line map: two ways to see what is left.
- **Mysteries to keep open until the end:** who sent the basket; why lightning struck the runestones that night; why the twins' eyes glow; why Asgard hangs over the lake. **Proposed answer:** the imprisoned gods' last act — they sent two soul-sparks down in the storm; the lightning woke the runestones so the twins could find their way (that is why runestones and gems light for them).
- **Painted panels** (estimate ~16): the opening (3), each realm's fall (4, one per realm — shown when its last spirit is freed), the orbs breaking, Odin and Freya revealed, the new Bifröst with the gods, the lightning and vines at the village, the land turning green, the celebration and time passing (2). Spirit memories and tablets stay text.

## 5. Keeping it fast

- **Soul-lines cost nothing per frame.** The master design is computed once from the world seed (a few hundred line segments, ~20 ms). Lit pieces are drawn *into the ground chunks when they are baked* — no new sprites, no masks (the round-66c lesson), no per-frame drawing.
- **A soft glow only near you:** the chunks around the hero get one faint additive glow picture each, baked once; it breathes with a single alpha tween.
- **Lighting a piece:** a temporary line grows for 3–4 s, then the few chunks it touches are re-baked in batches (four a frame, as the volcano carve already does).
- **The map:** lit pieces are drawn once into the map's canvas when they light; opening the map draws one image.
- **The green transformation:** tile changes along the lines and the new houses are applied in batches *during* the cut-scene, so the hitch is hidden; new villages spawn their pictures per chunk as you approach (like Asgard's city lots).
- **Odin and Freya** are loaded only in Asgard and the ending; cut-scene panels load one ahead; atlas pages are evicted as now.
- **Monsters gone** = their spawners off, which also makes free roam lighter.

## 9. The saga, as decided (Oct 19, round 67 — first draft)

**Answers that shape it:** the other twin stays in the village; the goddess is **Freya**; **Solmyrk is an impostor** who stole Odin's high seat, mask and names; the world is **Runeheim** (Muspelheim is Surtharn's fire realm); the familiars and fairy monarchs go with the spirits at the end and live only in legend after; monsters vanish everywhere; credits after the celebration; Pip and Wren are gone until the end; the story is told by the freed spirits, the builders and fairies, and the elders and the other twin; the twins are **the gods' last gift**; 30+ painted panels.

**Before (told in pieces, never in order):**
- **The cycle.** In Runeheim the dead rose along the World Tree, carved into the Runestone Green, to Asgard — Odin's Valhalla for the fallen, Freya's Fólkvangr for the rest — and new souls came down the same way into newborns.
- **Year 0 — the gate shuts.** Solmyrk, a thing of stolen light, came to Asgard wearing a golden mask. He caught Odin and Freya in two great orbs of their own light, sat in the high seat, took Odin's names, and shut Asgard's gate. No soul could rise; no new soul came down. The same night his ally **Surtharn** woke in Muspelheim: the **Ash Night** — fire and ash fell on the Ashlands, the **Ember Wall** rose against the Grasslands and the **Cinder Chasm** split from the Highlands. The Ashlands' people perished; their souls, with nowhere to go, were caught by Surtharn's **Binders** and chained to dig.
- **Year ~3 — the Stone-Fall.** The Highlands' mines were made to dig deeper for "a voice in the stone"; the mountains broke, the **Great Scarp** tore open, the Highlands fell.
- **Year ~8 — the Black Tide.** The Silverrun rose black from the south; the marshes drank the Wetlands' villages; the river became a wall. Only the Grasslands were left.
- **The Long Quiet.** No child was born alive in the last village. The dead of the Grasslands drifted at the edge of the light — until lately, when the Binders came over the walls for them too, and bindstones began to hum in the fields.
- **Year ~20 — the storm.** On the worst night anyone remembered, lightning struck runestone after runestone across the Grasslands. At dawn a floating basket drifted out of Mirror Lake to the harbour with two babes in it; their eyes glowed rune-blue until they were nursed on goat's milk. The village named them **Zeldara** and **Zeldor**.
- **Year ~36 — now.** The twins read runes no one else can, and runestones and gems light for them. The village, old and tired, sends one of them out; the other stays to keep the village and piece the old sagas together. Each one's spending brings wealth home — the village grows with every realm reopened.

**The game (as built, now with soul-lines):** the bindstones and their Binders; each freed spirit's soul-lines trace into the land; the builders freed reopen the crossings; the forges; Muspelheim and Surtharn; the escape; Asgard descending to the lake; the Bifröst; the citadel; **Solmyrk**.

**The end:**
1. Solmyrk falls; the citadel crumbles; the **feast hall**'s doors swing open.
2. Inside, two vast orbs float joined like an infinity sign; voices inside; the Lodestar trembles.
3. Godsbane into the centre: the orbs burst into millions of shards of light — **Odin and Freya**.
4. They thank the twin and tell the truth: they sent the twins down in the storm as their last act — two soul-sparks of their own light; the lightning woke the runestones so the twins could find their way.
5. At the heart of Asgard the Lodestar is set; the new Bifröst rises; Odin and Freya lead the spirits home — the warriors to Valhalla, the rest to Fólkvangr — and the familiars and the fairy monarchs go with them, into legend.
6. Back at the village centre: Odin's blue lightning, Freya's green vines; the soul-lines turn to grass and paths across the land; the monsters vanish; the forges sink; Asgard's shell sinks into the lake (a few spires above the water); villagers walk out on the new paths; houses rise in every realm.
7. The celebration of the twins, time passing, children born again (Pip and Wren among the first). Credits. Free roam.
