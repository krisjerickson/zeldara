## ★ The seasons option: the year that never turned

Since the gate shut, the year in the last village has been stuck in a grey late autumn: the leaves never quite fall, the frost never quite comes, the festivals are kept by habit. Opening each quarter of the world turns the wheel one season — the land, the light, the music, the villagers' clothes and moods change with it — until, after the end, Freya's true spring arrives with the first cry.

| When | The season |
|---|---|
| Before (the Long Quiet) | A grey, stuck late autumn. Dry leaves, low light, no frost, no snow. |
| The Grasslands open | The first frost, then deep winter: snow on the Green, breath in the air, Yule lights in windows. The year is moving again. |
| The Wetlands open | The thaw and the spring rains: floods going down, mud, the first green, birds back over the lake. |
| The Highlands open | High summer: long light, midsummer fires on the hills, haymaking, warm nights. |
| The Ashlands open | Autumn and harvest — a true one this time: falling leaves, Winter Nights, the dead remembered, ember-skies in the west. |
| After the end | Freya's spring, the real one: blossom along the soul-lines, lambs, bees back in Tob's hives, the first cradles. |

If you drop the seasons, the three seasonal arcs keep every chapter; only the weather, the festivals' dates and a few lines change. Other mappings work too (each realm its own season all year; or the seasons simply cycle with the day count).

## 1. The Homecomings ★

*Tender, bittersweet, hopeful* — Every household in the last village lost someone to the shut road — and every one of them is among the forty spirits you free. Each spirit you bring home is somebody's husband, sister, teacher or friend, and the village heals one empty chair at a time.

**Why it works:** It uses what the game already has: the forty spirits, their memories, and the backstories (Rolf's Hallveig, Nell's Eyvind and Dagny, Garrick's Thora, Benno's mother Yrsa…). Freeing spirits stops being a count and becomes a reunion you see on the Green.

**Where it meets the Saga:** Rides the spirits (Act II), the soul-lines and the farewell over the Bifröst (Act VI). The secret is kept: the villagers learn who the spirits are, never why the twins can free them.

**★ The seasons in it:** Each realm's homecomings turn a season: the first Grasslands spirits bring the first snow (Rolf's Hallveig comes home at Yule); the Wetlands' bring the thaw; the Highlands' come home on midsummer night by the hill fires; the Ashlands' at Winter Nights, the feast of the dead; and the farewell at the Bifröst is the last day of winter before Freya's spring.

**The risk:** Many sad beats — needs the cheerful villagers (Benno, Pia, Tilda) to stay funny around it.

## 2. The Red Gold

*Mystery, intrigue, redemption* — "The Binders paid in Muspelheim gold. Some of us took it. That's how it starts." Someone helped the Binders, long ago. As you free the realms, the clues come home with you — and the trail leads into the village itself.

**Why it works:** A whodunit gives every trip home a reason: you bring back a clue, villagers react, suspicion moves. It ties Morwen's teacher, Tilda's key, Coral's smuggling, Garrick's chains and Sigrid's memory into one thread.

**Where it meets the Saga:** Rides the bargain of fire and false light (Act II, beat 12). The buyer of the binding-song is the masked one's go-between — shown only as "a voice like a sun going out", never named, so the feast-hall secret holds.

### Prologue — A Debt of Eleven Silver (The first walk)

*Main Saga beneath it:* The opening

Tilda shows you the iron key on the nail behind her counter: an Ashlander left it as surety on the Ash Night and never came back for it. Garrick refuses to forge a chain for the well. Morwen locks her back room when you walk in.

Mini-chapters: **Tilda the Merchant** — The Ashlander's key; **Garrick the Blacksmith** — No chains; **Morwen the Sorcerer** — The locked back room.

✦ Tilda: "He owes me eleven silver. Thirty-six years of interest. If you find him, tell him."

### Chapter 1 — Red Gold in the Grass (The Grasslands)

*Main Saga beneath it:* The first Binders; the first spirits

A Binder's purse holds coins of strange red gold. Iva says the gold "was never dug — it was poured". Coral says she's seen it before, once, in the hands of a buyer at the harbour, the year the gate shut.

Mini-chapters: **Iva the Jeweler** — Poured gold; **Coral the Island Trader** — The buyer at the harbour; **Harbormaster Quill** — The old harbour ledger.

✦ Iva holds a red coin to her ear and goes white: "It's screaming."

Sigrid's memory comes home with her spirit. The village holds a moot on the Green: who took the gold? Suspicion falls on Coral (the smuggler), Quill (the land deals) and Fenwick (who knows too much about embers). Holt says Torvi heard the trees whisper a name.

Mini-chapters: **Coral the Island Trader** — Accused; **Harbormaster Quill** — Accused; **Ranger Holt** — The whispered name; **Elder Runa** — Calls the moot.

✦ The moot: you can speak for any of the accused — the village remembers who you stood up for.

### Chapter 3 — The Silver Tongue (The Highlands)

*Main Saga beneath it:* The Stone-Fall; "a voice in the stone"

Dunn's Book of Grudges names "the man with the silver tongue who sold the miners a song". Brask remembers the caravan he guarded forty years ago — and Zahir's locked chest. Morwen, cornered, tells you she once read the binding-song in her teacher's book.

Mini-chapters: **Dunn the Dwarf Forger** — Volume three, page one; **Sergeant Brask** — The caravan; **Morwen the Sorcerer** — What she read.

### Chapter 4 — The Counter-Verse (The Ashlands)

*Main Saga beneath it:* The forges; Muspelheim

Tilda's key opens Zahir's strongbox: the record of the sale, to a buyer "who paid in red gold and spoke with a voice like a sun going out" — and the Ashlander who left the key was the one who tried to steal it back. Mini-chapters: **Tilda the Merchant** — The key turns; **Morwen the Sorcerer** — The Counter-Verse; **Garrick the Blacksmith** — Thora's chains.

✦ Garrick, who has never forged a chain, breaks one with a single blow at a bindstone.

### Chapter 5 — Paid in Full (The end, and after)

*Main Saga beneath it:* The spirits go home

At the Bifröst a spirit stops beside Morwen and says she is forgiven. The red gold is melted into the bells of the new hamlets. Tilda gets her eleven silver — from you, as a joke — and writes your name on her REGULARS list.

Mini-chapters: **Morwen the Sorcerer** — Forgiven; **Tilda the Merchant** — Paid in full; **Coral the Island Trader** — Goes legal (mostly).

**What it takes to build:** A clue item per realm, a moot scene with a choice, the Counter-Verse draught (a chain-loosening item), suspicion lines for the suspects.

**The risk:** Darker, and the village turning on its own people needs a light touch (no villain among the villagers — the betrayer was the outsider Zahir).

## 3. The Turning of the Year ★

*Festive, warm, funny* — The village has kept its festivals by habit through thirty-six grey years. As each quarter of the world opens, the year turns — and each festival comes back to life with a feast, a contest and a mini-game, until the last one is the real spring.

**Why it works:** Turns the trips home into celebrations with something to do: the mini-games you picked (Runa's hnefatafl, Dunn's forge-off, the monarchs' feast), plus Rolf's flyting and the Benno–Morwen taste-off. The most playful arc, and the most "game".

**Where it meets the Saga:** Rides the realms opening (each builder freed = a festival) and the celebration (Act VI). Asgard and the gods only arrive at the last feast.

**★ The seasons in it:** This is the seasons arc at heart: each festival is the season the realm brings. Without seasons it still works as "four feasts", one per realm opened.

### Prologue — The Grey Harvest (The first walk)

*Main Saga beneath it:* The opening

It is "harvest" again, as it has been for thirty-six years: half a sheaf on the Green, a feast nobody enjoys, Fenwick lighting lamps at noon because the light is so dim. Benno is trying very hard. Oswin promises a real feast "when the list is shorter".

Mini-chapters: **Benno the Baker** — Trying very hard; **Old Fenwick** — Lamps at noon; **Master Oswin** — A promise.

✦ The village toasts with water. Rolf says it's the saddest thing he's ever poured.

### Chapter 1 — Yule (The Grasslands)

The first frost in thirty-six years, then snow. The village keeps a true Yule: the Yule log, lights in every window, and the Thaw-Moot game of hnefatafl against Elder Runa, who has not lost since she was nine.

Mini-chapters: **Elder Runa** — Hnefatafl at Yule; **Hana the Stable Master** — Bramble in the snow; **Tob the Farmer** — Naming the snowmen; **Watchman Corr** — A Yule poem for Ilsa, left at the gate.

### Chapter 2 — The Taste-Off (The Wetlands)

*Main Saga beneath it:* Bram's bridge; Mira's rescue

The thaw and the spring rains; the floods go down and the Silverrun runs clear. At the spring market Benno and Morwen settle "bread versus potion" at last, with you as judge. Marta is back with a real cart.

Mini-chapters: **Benno the Baker** — Old Mother's bread; **Morwen the Sorcerer** — Liquid bread, with footnotes; **Marta the Cart Vendor** — The river is a road again; **Greta the Miller** — The spring wind.

✦ Mini-game: the taste-off — pick the winner, and the loser sulks for a realm (with lines to match).

### Chapter 3 — Midsummer Fires (The Highlands)

*Main Saga beneath it:* Mira's lift; Dunn's rescue

High summer: fires on the hills, the longest day. Dunn and Garrick hold the forge-off by the bonfire, and Rolf defends his flyting crown against all comers — including you.

Mini-chapters: **Dunn the Dwarf Forger** — The forge-off; **Garrick the Blacksmith** — The forge-off; **Rolf the Bartender** — The flyting; **Mira the Mechanic** — A firework lift.

Mini-chapters: **Master Oswin** — Five tankards at the table; **Rolf the Bartender** — Two cups; **Ilsa the Lighthouse Keeper** — A light for the lake; **Old Fenwick** — Lamps for the dead.

✦ Mini-game: the monarchs' feast — seat the guests by their riddles.

### Chapter 5 — The Real Spring (The end, and after)

*Main Saga beneath it:* Lightning and vines; children again

Freya's spring along the soul-lines and the first true feast in thirty-six years: the celebration, honey cakes, the first cry, and Odin telling dad jokes at Rolf's bar.

Mini-chapters: **Freya** — The spring; **Odin** — Dad jokes at the bar; **Benno the Baker** — Honey cakes; **Pip** — Why is it spring?.

**The risk:** Lighter in tone than the main Saga — the Winter Nights chapter carries the grief so the arc does not feel weightless.

## 4. The Saga of the Last Village

*Witty, warm, a little meta* — Your twin is writing it all down. Every report you bring home becomes a chapter, every villager wants to be in it (bigger), and the saga everyone argues over has one verse missing — the one Vigdis the skald could never find.

**Why it works:** Makes your twin the narrator of the villagers' stories and gives every villager a funny stake: Oswin exaggerates, Bram corrects, Vela narrates herself, Corr wants to write the love scenes. A living codex of the game.

**Where it meets the Saga:** Rides the twin's scholarship (the string wall, the deductions, Godsbane) and Vigdis's missing verse "about who sits on the high seat" — which the twin only writes after the feast hall.

## 5. The Mending ★

*Hard-won, practical, communal* — The last village is breaking — a failing well, empty houses, the Binders' embers at the edge of the light at dusk. The one who stays keeps it going; the one who goes brings back what it needs. Realm by realm the village rebuilds, argues, defends itself, and finally sends its people out along the new roads.

**Why it works:** Makes the village itself change on screen as you play (houses relit, the mill turning, boats on the lake, new stalls) and gives the villagers jobs in the story — the builders, the smiths, the healers, the watch.

**Where it meets the Saga:** Rides "one goes, one stays" (Act I), the barriers and builders (Act II) and the new roads (Act VI). The Binders lately come over the walls for the Grasslands dead (canon) — that is the threat at home.

**★ The seasons in it:** The village works with the year: the first winter is survival (wood, the well, the night watch); the spring thaw is rebuilding; summer is the forge and the harvest; autumn is storing and defending; after the end, the spring the hamlets are founded in.
