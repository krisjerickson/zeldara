# Lily
key: lily
who: Lily
group: fairies
realm: 2
fairy: 0
kind: lily nymph
spirits: Liv, Magni
## Backstory
Lily rides a lotus petal and has a waterfall for hair, and she is the calmest creature in the Wetlands, which is saying something in a place
that drowned. She kept the stone at Millbrook, where the Silverrun turned the great mill wheel, and she watched the wheel turn backwards on
the night of the Black Tide. Her two are Liv, the young miller who was standing at the wheel when it happened, and Magni, the stonemason who
built Millbrook's dikes higher every year — until the water came up through instead of over. Magni blames himself. Lily has spent
twenty-eight winters telling him, gently, every single day, that it was not his fault, and has never once raised her voice.
## Before
- Liv was at the wheel when it turned backwards. She still hears it. I hum over the sound for her, every night.
- Magni built the dikes. He thinks the Tide came because his wall was not high enough. It came up from under. Nothing would have been high enough.
- I tell Magni it was not his fault. Every day. Some days he nearly believes me. Those are good days.
- Millbrook is under the reeds now. You can still see the top of the wheel when the water is low. Do not look at it too long.
- They are kind to each other, my two. Liv brings Magni the songs of the river. Magni builds Liv little walls of pebbles. In chains. Still.
## After
- Liv went straight to the river and put her hand in it. It ran the right way. She laughed until she cried.
- Magni looked at the dikes for a long time. Then he said, "I could make them better." That is the first time in twenty-eight winters he has wanted to build anything.
- The mill wheel is coming up out of the reeds. Bram helped. Magni would not let him do the stonework.
- I have nothing to hum over any more. I hum anyway. Habit.
- When they rose, Magni said, "It was not my fault." Not to me. To himself. I have been waiting twenty-eight winters for him to say that.
## Life
- I ride a lotus petal. It is the most comfortable thing in the world, and it never sinks if you are kind to it.
- My hair is a little waterfall. In winter it freezes and I am a very quiet fairy for a while.
- I am calm because the Wetlands needed someone to be. Everyone else was busy drowning.
- I have never raised my voice. I do not know if I can. I am a little afraid to find out.
- The other Wetlands fairies call me "the Pond". I do not mind. Ponds are deep.
## Stage 2
- The Silverrun runs clear past Millbrook. There is a heron fishing where the mill-race was. I have asked him to move. He has not.
## Stage 3
- The mill wheel turns again. The right way. Liv grinds the first flour herself. It is grey and gritty and the best bread in Runeheim.
## Stage 4
- Magni is building a new dike, very low. Not to keep the water out. Just to sit on and watch it.
## Stage 5
- Children paddle in the mill-race now. Liv shouts at them to be careful. Then she joins in.

# Marsh
key: marsh
who: Marsh
group: fairies
realm: 2
fairy: 1
kind: lily nymph
spirits: Nanna, Oddny
## Backstory
Marsh kept the stone in the reed-beds outside Reedhaven, and she is the gloomiest fairy in Runeheim and knows it. She says the worst thing
first so it cannot ambush her later. Her two are Nanna, a midwife whose hands had nothing to hold for six years before the Tide, and
Oddny, an old potter who fired urns for the dead that never cooled. Marsh noticed that the urns never cooled; that is how she first knew the
dead were not going home. She has been expecting the worst ever since, which has saved her a great deal of disappointment and none of the
grief.
## Before
- Nanna was a midwife for six years with nothing to catch. Then the Tide came. You can imagine. Do not imagine too hard.
- Oddny's urns never cooled. That is how I knew. The dead were not leaving. They were stuck, keeping the clay warm.
- The Binder at their stone used to run the Reedhaven ferry, they say. He took the fire-giant's coin and stopped asking where the boat went.
- I expect you will fail. I expect it of everyone. It is not personal. It is the Wetlands.
- If you do free them, I will expect them to be miserable. If they are not, I will be very pleasantly surprised.
## After
- You did it. I am very pleasantly surprised. Do not get used to it.
- Oddny touched an old urn and it went cold in her hands. She sat down in the reeds and laughed like a girl.
- Nanna keeps looking at her hands. She says they are ready. I asked, "Ready for what?" She smiled and would not say.
- I tried to expect the worst today and could not think of anything. It was very unsettling.
- Reedhaven had three thousand people. Now it has reeds. But the reeds are green. I will take green.
## Life
- I am the gloomiest fairy in Runeheim. I have checked. The others are all ridiculously hopeful.
- Say the worst thing first, and it cannot ambush you. That is my whole philosophy. It fits on a reed.
- Lily is calm. Ripple is cheerful. Nixie rings bells. I wait for things to go wrong. Every family needs one.
- I like fog. It hides everything, so nothing can disappoint you.
- I have a frog. Its name is Frog. It is the most reliable friend I have ever had.
## Stage 2
- The reed-beds are full of birds again. Loud ones. I did not ask for this. I like it.
## Stage 3
- Somebody is building in Reedhaven. Small houses on stilts. They will probably flood. I hope they do not.
## Stage 4
- Oddny is making new urns. Not for the dead. For flowers. She says the dead will not need them now. I think she is right. I hate being hopeful.
## Stage 5
- Nanna's hands are full now. She does not stop smiling. Neither do I, and I have tried.

# Ripple
key: ripple
who: Ripple
group: fairies
realm: 2
fairy: 2
kind: lily nymph
spirits: Ragna, Sigrid
## Backstory
Ripple is the cheerful one, which in the Wetlands took real effort. She kept the stone on the dike-top at Hverholm, the town behind the great
walls, and her two are Ragna, a shepherd girl who died young and who decided the gods were not angry but sad, and Sigrid, a young smith who
took the Binders' Muspelheim gold the year before the Tide and has never forgiven herself. Ripple made it her work to keep Ragna laughing and
to keep Sigrid from hating herself, and she has managed one of those, most days. She hides her own fear by skipping across the water.
## Before
- Ragna was a shepherd girl. She thinks the sky is not angry, just sad. I think she is cleverer than most grown-ups.
- Sigrid took the Binders' gold, once. Muspelheim gold. That is how it starts, she says. She says it every day, as if I might forget.
- I make Ragna laugh with ripples. Like this. See? Well, imagine it in the dark, with chains. It still works.
- Sigrid will not look at you. Not because of you. Because of the gold. Be patient with her.
- Hverholm's walls held the river out for a hundred years. The Tide came up through the floors. The walls are still standing. That is almost worse.
## After
- Ragna rose giggling. She asked if the sheep were all right. I said there are new sheep. She said that will do.
- Sigrid stood at the forge in Hverholm a long time. Then she lit it. She is making horseshoes. "Something useful," she says. "Something honest."
- I skipped across the whole Silverrun today and back. Nobody needed cheering up. I did it for fun. Imagine that.
- Sigrid looked at me today. At me. And said thank you. I fell off the water.
- Ragna wants to know if the sky is still sad. I told her to look up. She says it looks better. So do I.
## Life
- I am the cheerful one. It is not easy. You have to practise every morning, like a song.
- When I am frightened, I skip across the water. If you see ripples on a still pond, that was me, being brave.
- Hverholm was a town of walls and bells and ducks. The ducks are back. The bells are Nixie's business.
- I keep a pebble from every stone in the Wetlands. Fifty-three. They are all slightly wet.
- Marsh says I am ridiculously hopeful. I say Marsh is secretly hopeful and very bad at hiding it.
## Stage 2
- The ducks of Hverholm have come home. They are as rude as ever. I am delighted.
## Stage 3
- Sigrid's horseshoes are on every horse in the Wetlands. Hana says they are the best she has ever seen. Sigrid blushed. Smiths do not blush. She did.
## Stage 4
- Ragna asked me what is past the Ember Wall. I said fire. She said, "And after that?" I did not know. You will find out.
## Stage 5
- There is a new shepherd girl on the hill by Hverholm. Ragna sits beside her every evening and teaches her the songs.

# Nixie
key: nixie
who: Nixie
group: fairies
realm: 2
fairy: 3
kind: lily nymph
spirits: Torvi, Ulf
## Backstory
Nixie never slept. When the Black Tide drowned the Lantern Isles, their bell-towers went under still ringing, and Nixie decided they would
not stop: for twenty-eight winters she swam down every night and rang them by hand, so that the dead in the marsh would hear something that
was not the Binders' chains. She is half wild from it, and very loud, and a little deaf. Her two are Torvi, a forester who heard the marsh
trees whisper a name like a sun going out, and Ulf, a brewer from the isles whose offering-mead once turned to ash in the cup.
## Before
- I rang the drowned bells every night. Every one. Twenty-eight winters. My arms are very strong for a fairy. Feel. No, do not.
- Torvi heard the trees whisper a name. She will not say it to me. She says it is not a name for small creatures.
- Ulf brewed the best mead on the Lantern Isles. One year the cup turned to ash. He has not trusted a cup since.
- WHAT? Sorry. The bells. I do not hear very well. Say it again, louder, and with your hands.
- The Binders hate the bells. That is why I rang them. Anything a Binder hates is worth doing twice.
## After
- Torvi went into the marsh trees and listened. She says they are whispering something else now. A kinder thing. She will not say that either.
- Ulf brewed a little mead and poured the first cup on the ground. It did not turn to ash. He sat with it a long time.
- I do not have to ring the bells any more. I rang them anyway, once, to say they were free. The whole marsh heard.
- My arms are tired for the first time in twenty-eight winters. I am going to let them be tired.
- The Lantern Isles are coming up out of the water, one roof at a time. The bells were still there. Of course they were. I kept them.
## Life
- I never slept. Not once. Lily slept on her petal. Marsh slept in fog. I rang bells.
- Nixie means a water-sprite who drags sailors under. I have never dragged anyone. I have rescued four. Ask the herons.
- I am loud because the bells are louder. You would be loud too.
- I like thunderstorms. They are the only thing that can out-ring me.
- If you ever hear a bell under water, wave. It is probably me, saying hello.
## Stage 2
- The Lantern Isles' lights are lit again. One lantern per house. There are eleven houses so far. I have counted the lanterns more than once.
## Stage 3
- Ulf has opened a little mead-house on the first isle. He calls it The Bell. I asked him why. He winked.
## Stage 4
- Torvi says the trees have stopped whispering the dark name. They are whispering yours now. Do not let it go to your head.
## Stage 5
- The bells ring at every wedding on the isles. I ring them. Nobody has asked me to stop. I will not stop.

# Fenna
key: fenna
who: Fenna
group: fairies
realm: 2
fairy: 4
kind: lily nymph
spirits: Vigdis, Yrsa
## Backstory
Fenna is the scholar of the Wetlands fairies — she has read everything that ever floated past her stone, which, in a drowned land, is a great
deal. She kept the stone where the Silverrun meets the lake road, and her two are Vigdis, an old skald who knew every saga except one verse,
the one about who sits on the high seat, and Yrsa, a baker who sent bread up for the midsummer feast every year until the year no one came
to take it. Fenna and Vigdis have spent twenty-eight winters arguing about verses; Yrsa has spent them describing bread, which Fenna has never
tasted and has strong opinions about.
## Before
- Vigdis knows every saga ever sung, except one verse. The one about the high seat. It drives her mad. It drives me mad too, and I am very patient.
- Yrsa baked bread for the midsummer feast every year. The last year, nobody came down to take it up. She left it on the hill. It is still there in her mind.
- Vigdis and I argue about verses. I am usually right. She is usually louder. In a saga, louder wins.
- Yrsa describes bread to me every night. I have never eaten bread. I am an expert anyway.
- The missing verse. If you ever find it, tell Vigdis before you tell anyone. She has earned it.
## After
- Vigdis rose reciting. She has not stopped. She has a great deal to catch up on, and an audience at last.
- Yrsa went straight to an oven in the nearest standing house and baked. I have now tasted bread. I was right about everything.
- Vigdis says she will find the missing verse herself now. I believe her. I also believe your twin will find it first. Do not tell her I said so.
- Yrsa left a loaf on the hill again, for midsummer. "In case," she said. I did not ask in case of what.
- My stone has never been so quiet. Vigdis is reciting in the village now. I miss the arguing. I have started arguing with herons.
## Life
- I have read everything that ever floated past my stone. Recipes, letters, half a saga, three tax-rolls and a love poem to a goat.
- Fenna is the fen — the wet, flat, patient land. I am patient. I am also usually right, which makes the patience hard.
- I keep the floating pages in a dry hollow under the stone. It is the only library in the Wetlands. You may visit. Wipe your feet.
- I do not like fire. I have too many pages.
- The love poem to the goat is quite good. I think the goat said yes.
## Stage 2
- Vigdis has taken three apprentices in the village. Grown men, all of them, and she makes them recite until they weep. They come back every day.
## Stage 3
- Yrsa sent bread up the hill at midsummer and it was gone in the morning. She thinks it was a fox. I have my own theory. I am keeping it.
## Stage 4
- I have started writing the Wetlands' own saga, from the pages I saved. Vigdis says my verses are too long. Vigdis is not wrong.
## Stage 5
- Vigdis has the missing verse now. She recited it on the Green and nobody breathed. I cried on my lotus. Lily says I made the pond salty.
