# Liv
key: sp_liv
who: Liv, the miller of Millbrook
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a miller
## Backstory
The young miller of Millbrook, standing at the great wheel on the Silverrun on the night of the Black Tide, when the river ran black from the
south and the wheel turned backwards. Lily kept her stone by the drowned mill.
## Life
- The Millbrook wheel was the biggest in Runeheim. Forty paddles. When it turned, the floor of the whole town hummed.
- I was the youngest miller Millbrook ever had. My father's knees went, and the wheel does not wait for anyone's knees.
- On the night of the Tide the wheel stopped, groaned, and turned the wrong way. I stood there with the flour sack in my arms. I did not drop it.
- Lily says the wheel is still down there under the water, turning backwards very slowly. I would like to see it turn the right way once.
- Flour on your hands, the river in your ears, bread in every oven down the road. That is what a mill is for. I would like to be for something again.

# Magni
key: sp_magni
who: Magni, the stonemason of Millbrook
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a stonemason
## Backstory
A stonemason of Millbrook who spent every summer raising the great dikes at Hverholm, a foot higher each year. The water never came over them;
it came up through the ground, and he has never stopped working out where his stones went wrong.
## Life
- Every summer we raised the Hverholm dikes another foot. Every summer the old men said, that is enough now, Magni. It was never enough.
- A good wall is a conversation between the stones. Each one leans a little on its neighbour. Like a town, if the town is any good.
- The water did not come over. It came up through. Through the fields, through the floors, through the very road. No wall can stop that.
- I have measured that night in my head a thousand times. I think now there was no stone I could have laid. That helps, a little.
- When the Wetlands dry I will build something that is not a wall. A bridge, maybe. Something that lets people across instead of keeping things out.

# Nanna
key: sp_nanna
who: Nanna, the midwife of Reedhaven
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a midwife
## Backstory
The midwife of Reedhaven in the reed-beds, whose hands had nothing to do for six long years before the Black Tide took the town. Marsh kept
her stone and her company.
## Life
- In Reedhaven every door knew my knock. Day or night, rain or ice. I had the warmest hands in the Wetlands, they said.
- For six years nobody needed me. I kept my bag packed anyway. A midwife who unpacks her bag has given up.
- I helped the potters. I helped the reed-cutters. I sat with the old ones at the end. There is always someone who needs hands.
- Marsh is gloomy, but she is kind. She said the worst things first so they could not jump out at us later. It works, you know.
- My bag is still packed. I do not know what for. But I have a feeling I should keep it near.

# Oddny
key: sp_oddny
who: Oddny, the potter of Reedhaven
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a potter
## Backstory
An old potter of Reedhaven who fired the urns for the dead of the reed-beds. She noticed, years before anyone, that the urns never cooled —
that something was keeping the dead from rising.
## Life
- Reedhaven clay is grey and fine, and it fires the colour of the sky before rain. Every house in the Wetlands drank from my cups.
- An urn should cool by morning. Mine stayed warm for days, then weeks. I told the town. They said I was getting old. I was. I was also right.
- Marsh says I am the only person who ever made her laugh. I said something about a pot. I have forgotten what. She has not.
- The Tide filled my kiln with water. I expect it is full of fish now. That would be a fine thing for a kiln to have become.
- My urns are cold now, everywhere. I can feel it. Everyone who was waiting has gone up at last. That is the best thing I ever made.

# Ragna
key: sp_ragna
who: Ragna of Hverholm
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a shepherd girl
## Backstory
A shepherd girl of Hverholm, behind the great dikes, who died young the year before the Tide. Her mother said the gods were angry; Ragna decided
they were sad, and Ripple made it her work to keep her laughing.
## Life
- I had nine sheep and one of them was a goat who thought he was a sheep. I never told him. It would have hurt his feelings.
- On top of the dike you could see all the way to the lake one side and all the way to the hills the other. That was my favourite place in the world.
- Ripple tells the worst jokes in Runeheim. I laugh at all of them. Somebody has to.
- Mother said the sky was angry. I don't think the sky gets angry. I think it gets lonely, like a sheep that is lost.
- When the Wetlands dry I want to find where my sheep went. Sheep are very bad at staying put, but they are very good at being found.

# Sigrid
key: sp_sigrid
who: Sigrid, the smith of Hverholm
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a smith
## Backstory
A young smith of Hverholm who took the Binders' Muspelheim gold the year before the Tide — paid to forge iron rings she did not ask the use of.
She has never forgiven herself, and does not want anyone to say she should.
## Life
- Hverholm iron was the best for anything that had to stand in water. Hinges, chains, the dike gates. My forge was by the north wall.
- They paid in Muspelheim gold. It was warm in the hand, even in winter. That should have told me everything.
- I forged them rings. A hundred iron rings, heavy, with a hole for a pin. I did not ask what for. You know what for. I was chained by one.
- Ripple tells me I was young and the gold was a lot. Both true. Neither one is an answer.
- If there is a forge left in the Wetlands, I would like to make something that opens. Gates, locks, hinges. Things that open.

# Torvi
key: sp_torvi
who: Torvi, the forester of the Lantern Isles
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a forester
## Backstory
A forester of the Lantern Isles, who kept the marsh woods between the bell-towers. Before the Tide she heard the marsh trees whispering a name —
not a god's name, but one that sounded like a sun going out.
## Life
- On the Lantern Isles the trees grow out of the water on their roots, like old women holding up their skirts. I knew every one.
- Each tower had its bell, and each bell had its own note. On a still evening you could play a tune by walking from island to island.
- The trees whispered for a whole summer before the Tide. A name. A cold, slow name. I have never said it out loud and I never will.
- Nixie rang our bells every night under the water. I heard them through the stone. She is a little deaf now. It was worth it, she says.
- When the water goes down I will plant the first new tree on the Isles myself. Roots first. Always roots first.

# Ulf
key: sp_ulf
who: Ulf, the brewer of the Lantern Isles
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a brewer
## Backstory
The brewer of the Lantern Isles, whose mead was carried to every feast of the Wetlands. One year the first cup, poured for the one on the high
seat as it always was, turned to ash in his hand.
## Life
- Lantern Isles mead was made with marsh honey and a little smoke. People said it tasted like a summer evening. They were right.
- Every year we poured the first cup for the high seat. Every year it was the proudest moment of my life. Then the cup turned to ash.
- I did not brew again after that. I thought the mead had done something wrong. It had not. The world had.
- Nixie and I sang drinking songs to keep the dark away. Without the drinking. It is harder than it sounds.
- Rolf's ale is not bad, for a Grasslander. Tell him I said so. Tell him also that I will show him how to do it properly.

# Vigdis
key: sp_vigdis
who: Vigdis, the skald of Millbrook
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a skald
## Backstory
An old skald who sang in the hall at Millbrook where the lake road meets the Silverrun. She knew every saga of Runeheim but one verse — the one
about who sits on the high seat — and Fenna read to her through the dark years.
## Life
- A skald carries the whole world in her head. Every hero, every feud, every winter. It is heavy. You get used to it.
- There was always one verse missing. The one about the high seat. My teacher did not have it either. Nor hers.
- Fenna read me everything that floated past our stone. Shopping lists, love letters, a book about eels. I know a great deal about eels now.
- I am making a new saga. Do not be alarmed. You are in it. So is the fairy. So are the eels.
- One day someone will sing the missing verse. I hope I am in the hall when they do. I want to see the old skalds' faces.

# Yrsa
key: sp_yrsa
who: Yrsa, the baker of Millbrook
group: spirits
realm: 2
kind: a baker
## Backstory
A baker of Millbrook, on the lake road, who every midsummer sent bread up for the great feast — until the year no one came to carry it. She baked
it anyway, and it went stale on the step.
## Life
- Millbrook flour, Reedhaven salt, Hollowmere honey. My midsummer bread had a little of every town in it. That was the point.
- The year no one came for the feast bread, I left it on the step for three days. The birds ate well. I did not.
- Benno in the last village was my apprentice for one summer. He laughed at every joke I made. They were not good jokes. He is a good man.
- Fenna says my bread smelled so good the fish came up to the surface. Fenna exaggerates. Slightly.
- If I ever get near an oven again I am making midsummer bread, whatever the season. Someone will carry it up this time.
