## Backstory
The first fairy of the Grasslands and the bossiest. Primrose kept the stone by the Fernby crossroads, where farm carts once stopped to rest.
When the farms emptied in the Long Quiet she was left with nobody to boss but the dead: Asa, an old weaver from Fernby who was buried in a
birth-shawl, and Bersi, a boy who died the first winter and waited at the edge of the light. She made them a schedule. Morning: Asa tells a
story. Noon: Bersi says what he would build. Dusk: everyone sings. She has kept it every day for thirty-six winters and is quietly terrified
that once they are free nobody will need a schedule at all.
## Before
- Asa is chained at the old Fernby stone. Morning is her story time. Do not be late — she hates when I am late.
- Bersi was going to be a shipwright. He drew boats in the dirt for me. Thirty-six winters of boats, and not one of them ever got wet.
- They will not know you. Say my name first. Then say "Fernby". Then whatever else you like.
- I tried to bite the chain once. Fairy teeth are for honey. I do not recommend it.
- Asa wove twenty shawls for babies that never came. She is not sad about it any more. I am, a little, for her.
## After
- Asa came to my stone this morning and told her story at exactly the right time. I cried. Do not tell her.
- Bersi wants to see the lake. I told him boats float on it. He did not believe me, so I showed him. He has not stopped grinning.
- They do not need my schedule any more. I made a new one anyway. It has more naps in it.
- When they rose I tidied the stone. It did not need tidying. I just did not know what to do with my hands.
- Now that they are free, who do I sing to at dusk? You, I suppose. Stand still.
## Life
- I am the first fairy of the Grasslands. That means I was here first. It does not mean I am the oldest. Never say I am the oldest.
- Before the quiet, carts stopped at my stone and the farmers left a crust for me. I miss the crusts more than I should.
- I slept in a primrose for eleven winters. Yellow is a very loud colour to sleep inside.
- Clover is my sister-fairy. She counts leaves. I count everything else.
- The moss on the runes grows back every morning. I scrape it off every morning. We have an understanding, the moss and I.
- I am not afraid of wolves. I am afraid of being forgotten. Wolves are just very large and rude.
## Stage 2
- The carts are coming back to the crossroads. One farmer left a whole bun. I have named him my favourite person.
## Stage 3
- I flew to the next stone today and back. It took all morning. I used to do it before breakfast. I am out of practice at being free.
## Stage 4
- Fernby has smoke in a chimney again. Only one chimney. That is how everything starts.
## Stage 5
- The fields are full of children's voices. I have made a schedule for them. They do not follow it. I love them anyway.

# Bramble
key: bramble
who: Bramble
group: fairies
realm: 1
fairy: 1
kind: clover pixie
spirits: Dagny, Eyvind
## Backstory
Bramble kept the stone on the lake path below Stonewick, where the fishing families walked down to Mirror Lake each morning. She is prickly,
quick to anger and quicker to defend anyone she has decided is hers. Her two are Dagny, a net-mender, and Eyvind, a fisher — husband and wife
from Stonewick, bound at stones a hill apart so they could see each other's light but never reach. For thirty-six winters Bramble flew
messages between them, one word at a time, because that is all a fairy can carry in the dark. Dagny saw something on the water the night of
the storm; Bramble has never told anyone what Dagny said, and gets thorny if you ask.
## Before
- Dagny and Eyvind. Married. Bound a hill apart, and each can see the other's light. That is the cruellest thing I know.
- I carried one word a night between them. Thirty-six winters of words. Most of them were "still here".
- Eyvind mended nets and told lies about fish. Dagny mended nets and told the truth about everything. They were very happy.
- Free Dagny first, or Eyvind first — it does not matter. Just free them on the same day. Promise me.
- Do not ask me what Dagny saw on the water that night. That is hers to tell.
## After
- They walked to each other the moment the chains fell. Neither of them said anything. They did not need to; I had carried enough words.
- Eyvind asked me for one more message. I said, "She is standing right there." He said, "I know. Tell her anyway."
- I have no letters to carry now. My wings feel strange. Light. Useless. Lovely.
- Dagny told you what she saw, did she not? Good. Now you know why I am fond of you.
- Stonewick's lake path is just a path again. I walk it on foot sometimes, to feel ordinary.
## Life
- I am called Bramble because I am sharp and I hold on. Both are compliments. Say thank you.
- The fishers used to leave a sprat on my stone at dawn. I do not eat sprats. I liked that they remembered.
- Primrose makes schedules. I make thorns. Between us the Grasslands got through.
- I fought a crow once, for a button. I won. The crow will tell you otherwise. The crow is a liar.
- I was not afraid in the dark. I was angry in the dark. Anger lasts longer.
## Stage 2
- The lake is giving fish again. The fishers are back at dawn. One of them left a sprat. I still do not eat sprats. I said thank you anyway.
## Stage 3
- Stonewick's nets are hung out to dry on the wall again. Dagny inspects every one. Nobody dares tie a bad knot near her.
## Stage 4
- I flew to the top of the hill between their stones today. You can see the whole lake from there. They should build a bench. I will tell Bram.
## Stage 5
- There is a baby in Stonewick called Bramble. Her mother says it is after the hedge. I know better.

# Clover
key: clover
who: Clover
group: fairies
realm: 1
fairy: 2
kind: clover pixie
spirits: Frida, Geir
## Backstory
Clover kept the stone in the orchards above Hollowmere, among the beehives and the carving sheds. She counts things — leaves, bees, days —
because counting kept her sure she was still there, and she has the exact number of every day since the gate shut. Her two are Frida, a
beekeeper whose bees left the year the sky went quiet, and Geir, an old carver who as a boy carved the tree on the village Green. Clover
was the one who noticed the bees go, the first sign anything was wrong, and nobody listened to a fairy counting bees. She has been a little
bit right about everything ever since and finds it exhausting.
## Before
- Frida had forty-one hives. On the morning the sky went quiet there were forty-one, and by dusk there were none. I counted.
- Geir carved the tree on your Green when he was a boy. He is very proud of the roots. Ask him about the roots. Then sit down; it takes a while.
- Thirteen thousand one hundred and forty-something days. I lost count once, in the sixth winter. It frightened me more than the Binders.
- Frida asks me every evening if the bees are back. Every evening I say "not yet". I would like to say something else.
- Geir carves in the air with his fingers when he thinks nobody is looking. He is carving the same thing over and over: a door.
## After
- The bees came back the morning after Frida rose. All forty-one hives' worth. I counted twice to be sure.
- Geir asked to see the Green. He looked at the tree he carved for a very long time and said, "The roots go further than anyone thinks."
- I have stopped counting days. Now I count bees. It is much more cheerful, and they hold still less.
- Frida gave me a drop of the first honey. It is the best thing I have ever tasted, and I have tasted dew.
- Geir is carving a real door now, in Hollowmere. He will not say where it goes. I think it goes home.
## Life
- I count things. Leaves, bees, steps, stars. If I can count it, it is real. If I cannot count it, I worry.
- Four. A clover has four leaves. Always four. That is the most comforting number there is.
- I told the village the bees were leaving. Nobody listens to a fairy counting bees. They should.
- Primrose says I worry too much. Primrose was asleep in a flower for eleven winters. I was awake for all of mine.
- The orchards above Hollowmere had nine hundred and twelve trees. Three hundred are still standing. I visit each one.
## Stage 2
- The orchard has blossom on a hundred and six trees. Last spring it was none. I am trying very hard not to count it again.
## Stage 3
- Frida has started a forty-second hive. She says it is for luck. Luck does not need honey. I let her have it.
## Stage 4
- Geir's door in Hollowmere has a carved tree on it. The roots go all the way to the hinges.
## Stage 5
- I counted the children on the Green today. I got a different number every time. They keep moving. It is wonderful.

# Linnet
key: linnet
who: Linnet
group: fairies
realm: 1
fairy: 3
kind: clover pixie
spirits: Hallveig, Ingolf
## Backstory
Linnet is a singer. She kept the stone on the Barrow Downs road, where the old songs were sung over the dead on their way up the Tree, and
she knows every one of them. Her two are Hallveig, a cooper and Rolf's wife from the last village — the only one of her charges who ever
lived there — and Ingolf, a young tanner from Stonewick who learned, chained beside a bindstone, more about the Binders than anyone should.
Linnet sang to them every night so they would not forget the tunes they had lived to. She has never sung for anyone alive, and the idea
makes her so shy she goes the colour of a poppy.
## Before
- Hallveig is Rolf's wife. Yes, that Rolf. He still sets two cups out at the tavern. She knows. I told her.
- Ingolf talks about the Binders a great deal. He learned things, chained there. Listen to him. Then do not listen too long.
- I sing to them every night. The old songs, the ones for the road up. They cannot take the road. They can still have the songs.
- Hallveig's laugh is the loudest thing on the Downs. Even in chains. Especially in chains.
- Ingolf says the Binders do not come from the Ashlands but from under it. He says it the way you say a thing you wish you had not learned.
## After
- Hallveig went straight to the tavern. Rolf dropped both cups. Nobody has ever seen the tavern so quiet, or so loud right after.
- Ingolf is tanning hides again in Stonewick. He keeps the windows open. He says he has had enough of the dark for one lifetime.
- I sang the road-song when they rose. My first time singing for someone who could answer. Hallveig sang the harmony. Badly. Perfectly.
- Rolf brought me a thimble of mead. I am a very small fairy. I was very happy for the rest of the day.
- Now that they are home, I sing for the stone. It does not clap, but it hums back.
## Life
- I know every song the Downs ever sang over the dead. Two hundred and six. Clover counted them for me.
- I have never sung for anyone alive. If you ask me to, I will turn red and fly into a hedge.
- A linnet is a small brown bird with a red cap that sings more than it should. I chose my name very carefully.
- The barrows on the Downs are old, older than the stones. The dead there were already home when the gate shut. I envy them.
- When the Binders came along the road, I hid in the long grass and sang very, very quietly. They did not hear. Or they did not care.
## Stage 2
- A shepherd on the Downs was whistling one of my songs today. Wrong, but recognisably. I nearly fell off the stone.
## Stage 3
- Hallveig has taught the whole tavern the harmony to the road-song. They sing it at closing. I sit on the rafter and listen.
## Stage 4
- The road up the Downs is green again. When the road up the Tree opens too, I will sing every one of the two hundred and six. All the way.
## Stage 5
- I sang for the children on the Green today. In front of everyone. I went red. They clapped. I would like to do it again tomorrow.

# Sorrel
key: sorrel
who: Sorrel
group: fairies
realm: 1
fairy: 4
kind: clover pixie
spirits: Jorunn, Ketil
## Backstory
Sorrel slept thirty-six winters in a clover and counted its leaves every morning to be sure she was still herself — four, always four. She is
the smallest of the Grasslands fairies and the most matter-of-fact, the kind who says the frightening thing out loud so it gets smaller. She
kept the stone by the dye-pools of the Fernby water, and her two are Jorunn, a dyer who could match any blue in the world, and Ketil, a
goat-herder whose goats would not stop crying when he died. Sorrel kept Jorunn's blue alive by describing it to her every day, and Ketil's
goats by imitating them, badly, until he laughed.
## Before
- Thirty-six winters I slept in a clover. Do you know how small a clover is? I counted its leaves every morning to be sure I was still there.
- Jorunn could match any blue in the world. I describe the sky to her every morning so she does not forget which one is hers.
- Ketil's goats cried for a whole winter after he died. Then they wandered off. I do the goat voices for him. He says I am terrible at it.
- They are tired, my two. Not sad. Tired is worse, I think. Sad still wants something.
- If you are frightened of the Binder, say so. Saying it out loud makes it smaller. That is the only magic I have.
## After
- Jorunn stood in the dye-pools up to her knees and looked at the sky until it got dark. She says the blue is right. I knew it would be.
- Ketil has found three goats already. They are not his goats. He says that does not matter; goats are goats.
- I do not have to do the goat voices any more. I still do them. Ketil still says I am terrible. He laughs every time.
- When they rose, they were not tired any more. They wanted things. Breakfast. Goats. Blue. That is how I knew they were alive in the way that counts.
- I slept in a real bed of moss last night, not a clover. It was too big. I have gone back to the clover.
## Life
- Four. Always four leaves. That is the only reason I am still me.
- I am the smallest fairy in the Grasslands. The others say "smallest" like it means "least". It means "hardest to catch".
- Sorrel is a leaf that tastes sour. I chose it so nobody would try to eat me.
- When I am frightened I say the frightening thing out loud. "The Binder is very large." See? Now he is only large.
- The dye-pools by the Fernby water are every colour at once when the sun hits them. That is my favourite thing in the realm.
## Stage 2
- The dye-pools are being used again. Somebody in Fernby is wearing Jorunn's blue. I followed them for a mile to look at it.
## Stage 3
- Ketil's goats have had kids. Goat kids, not the other sort. He has named one after me. It eats everything. I am flattered.
## Stage 4
- The other fairies talk about the gate opening as if it is a door. I think it will be more like waking up. You do not open waking up.
## Stage 5
- I have stopped counting the leaves. I still know there are four. It is enough to know.
