## Backstory
Flint is hard, sparky and quick to strike — the Highlands fairy who kept the stone above Deepdelve, the deepest mine-town in Runeheim. She
heard the stone singing in the year before the Stone-Fall, the same song the miners heard, and told them not to follow it. They did not
listen to a fairy either. Her two are Arnor, a weaver who wove the miners' cloth and who heard the singing too, and Bodil, an old
shipwright who built ships on the peaks for a sea she could see from the top of the world — until the Scar took the road to the sea. Flint
keeps them warm by striking sparks, one at a time, all night.
## Before
- Arnor heard the stone singing. So did I. I told the miners. Nobody listens to a fairy who sparks when she talks.
- Bodil built ships on a mountain. For a sea she could see from the peaks. That is the bravest foolish thing I have ever known.
- I strike sparks to keep them warm. All night. Every night. My knuckles are very sore. Do not hold my hand.
- Do not listen if the stone sings to you down there. It sings the same song it sang to Deepdelve.
- Arnor weaves in the air with his fingers. Miners' cloth. Thick, grey, warm. He is weaving for men who are not coming home.
## After
- Arnor asked about the miners. I told him some of them are spirits too, in the other realms' stones. He has gone looking.
- Bodil climbed to the highest peak and looked west. The sea is still there. She says the road to it can be mended. Mira says the same.
- I stopped striking sparks. It is cold. I like it. Cold means nobody needs me to be warm for them.
- Deepdelve is still a hole in the mountain. Arnor says holes can be filled with better things. I do not know what he means. I hope he does.
- When they rose, the stone hummed. Not sang. Hummed. A good sound. I have been listening to it all week.
## Life
- I am called Flint because I spark when I am cross, and I am cross a great deal.
- I heard the singing first. I knew first. Being right first is lonely.
- Crystal pixies are hard to break. That is a good thing to be, in the Highlands.
- I keep a piece of the old Deepdelve gate in my stone. Black iron. I do not know why. I cannot let go of it.
- Aster says I should strike fewer sparks and talk more softly. Aster has never had to keep two people warm all night.
## Stage 2
- The Highland lift runs. Mira's cage rattles past my stone every morning. I spark at it. It is our greeting.
## Stage 3
- Bodil's first new boat is finished. It is on top of a mountain. She says it is "waiting". I like her more every day.
## Stage 4
- The stone in Deepdelve has stopped singing. Arnor went down to listen. He came up smiling. He would not say why.
## Stage 5
- Bodil's boat is on the western sea at last. She sent a message back with a gull. The gull bit me. Worth it.

# Aster

## Backstory
Aster is the gentle Highlands fairy, a star-shaped little crystal who kept the stone above the mountain lakes of Kettlecrag, where on still
nights you could see the great white city's reflection in the water. Her two are Dalla, a net-mender who knows of a chamber under the
mountains where light is chained like hounds, and Einar, a young fisher who used to watch the city in the lake until, one night, all he saw
was a golden mask. Aster has spent thirty-six winters telling them stories with happy endings, because she believes — fiercely, the only
fierce thing about her — that ending a story well is a kind of magic.
## Before
- Einar used to see the white city in the mountain lakes on still nights. Then one night only a golden mask, looking back up at him. He has not looked since.
- Dalla knows of a chamber under the mountains where light is chained. She will tell you. Listen closely. It matters.
- I tell them stories with happy endings. Every night. They say I am childish. They listen to every word.
- Kettlecrag fished the high lakes for a thousand years. The fish are still there. Nobody to catch them.
- If you find the chained light, be kind to it. Dalla says it has been afraid for a very long time.
## After
- Einar looked into the mountain lake last night. Just his own face. He said that was enough for now.
- Dalla told you about the chamber. Then she sat down and slept for a whole day. Some things are heavy to carry until you hand them over.
- I told them a story when they rose. It had a happy ending. This time it was true.
- Kettlecrag has a boat on the lake again. One boat. Einar's. He does not fish. He just floats.
- I do not have to make up happy endings now. You are making one. Do not stop.
## Life
- Aster means star. I am a very small star. I do my best.
- Flint sparks. I glow. Between us, the Highlands are well lit.
- I believe that if you end a story well, the world listens. I have no proof. I do not need any.
- I once tried to tell a Binder a story. He did not stop to listen. I told it anyway, to his back. I hope some of it got in.
- I am afraid of the dark under the mountains. That is why I sit at the top of them.
## Stage 2
- The Wetlands' bells rang all night when the Highland lift opened. Nixie, probably. I could hear them from here. I cried a little.
## Stage 3
- Einar caught a fish today. He let it go. He says the lake has done enough giving for a while.
## Stage 4
- Dalla went down to the chamber under the mountains. It is empty now. The light is gone. Gone home, she says.
## Stage 5
- On still nights the white city shows in the mountain lakes again. Einar takes his whole village out on the water to see it.

# Quartz

who: Quartz

fairy: 2

spirits: Gudrun, Hakon
## Backstory
Quartz is the practical Highlands fairy, clear-headed and clear all through — you can see the light come out the other side of her. She
kept the stone at Highmark, the last town on the cliff road to the western sea, and her two are Gudrun, a beekeeper whose golden queen bee
the Binders crushed first, and Hakon, a carver who noticed that every rune in the mountains was carved pointing toward the lake. Quartz spent
the dark years making maps, with Hakon's help, of every rune in the Highlands, because she is the kind of fairy who answers fear with a list.
## Before
- Hakon says every rune in the mountains points to the lake. He is right. I have mapped all of them. Two hundred and eight, all pointing home.
- Gudrun's queen bee was golden. The Binders crushed her first. Gudrun has not said a sharp word since. That is how I know how angry she is.
- I answer fear with lists. Here is today's: Binder, chain, cold, stone. Now it is a list. Lists can be crossed off.
- Highmark stood on the cliff road to the western sea. The road broke in the Stone-Fall. The town held on to the cliff a while. Then it did not.
- If you free Hakon, ask to see the rune map. Then you will understand why the lake matters so much.
## After
- Hakon and I finished the rune map. Two hundred and nine. We had missed one, under a waterfall. He found it on his first day free.
- Gudrun has bees again — wild ones, from the high meadows. She has not found a golden queen. She says she does not need one now.
- I made a list when they rose. It said: free. That is all. One word. The best list I have ever made.
- Highmark is a ruin on a cliff. Bodil says the sea-road can be mended. I have started a list of what that needs. It is long.
- The runes point to the lake. You are from the lake. I have added you to the map.
## Life
- You can see right through me. That makes me very bad at secrets and very good at windows.
- Quartz is the commonest crystal in the mountains. Common things hold the world up.
- I have a map of every rune in the Highlands, every stone, every spring and every hiding place. It took thirty-six winters. Ask, if you get lost.
- I do not like surprises. I like lists. Surprises are things that are not on lists yet.
- Heather says I should feel things more. I do feel things. I just write them down first.
## Stage 2
- I have added the lift to the map. And Mira. And Mira's hat. It is a very good hat.
## Stage 3
- Gudrun's wild bees have made honey that tastes of snow. I did not know honey could do that. It is now on my list of good things.
## Stage 4
- The rune map shows a new line, faint, running from the lake to the Ashlands. It was not there before. It is a soul-line. Yours.
## Stage 5
- The sea-road to Highmark is open. I walked it with Bodil and Hakon. I crossed off the last thing on the list. I cried. That was not on the list.

# Heather
key: heather
who: Heather
group: fairies
realm: 3
fairy: 3
kind: crystal pixie
spirits: Ingrid, Joreid
## Backstory
Heather is the soft one of the Highlands, all purple bloom and feeling, the fairy who kept the stone on the high moor where the Kettlecrag
families buried their own. Her two are Ingrid, an old cooper who saw the Great Scar split three winters after the gate shut and believes it
was the mountain answering the fire, and Joreid, the last child born in the Highlands, who lived three summers. Heather has loved Joreid like
her own for thirty-six winters and made her a world out of moor-flowers inside the stone's shadow. She is the fairy most afraid of the day
the chains break, because Joreid might go up the Tree without her.
## Before
- Joreid was the last child born in the Highlands. She lived three summers. She has been three summers old in chains for thirty-six winters.
- I made her a garden in the shadow of the stone, out of moor-flowers. She names every one. Some of the names are very rude.
- Ingrid saw the Scar split. She says the mountain was answering the fire. She is old and she is right about most things.
- I am afraid of the day you free them. Joreid will be free to go up the Tree. I want that. I do not want it. Both.
- Ingrid keeps Joreid warm with stories of barrels. Joreid finds barrels very boring. She listens anyway. That is love.
## After
- Joreid ran. Three summers old and thirty-six winters chained, and the first thing she did was run, straight through my garden. I have never seen anything better.
- Ingrid sat on the moor and watched the Scar for a long time. Mira's lift went past. Ingrid said, "Well. The mountain answered. Now we have."
- Joreid has not gone up the Tree yet. She says she is waiting for the road to be ready. I think she is waiting for me to be ready.
- I am trying to be ready. It is the hardest thing I have ever tried.
- Ingrid made Joreid a tiny barrel. Joreid says it is the least boring barrel in the world. Ingrid has never been so proud.
## Life
- I am heather: small, purple and stubborn enough to grow on a mountain. Most people think I am soft. They are half right.
- I feel things first and think about them later. Quartz does it the other way round. We argue beautifully.
- The moor where I keep my stone is where the Kettlecrag families buried their own. I know every name on every cairn.
- My garden in the stone's shadow has thirty-one kinds of flower. Joreid named all of them. "Sir Bossy" is a thistle.
- If I could have one wish, it would be for Joreid to grow up. I know I cannot have it. I wish it anyway, every night.
## Stage 2
- The lift has brought visitors to the moor. They left flowers on the cairns. I know whose. I thanked them, though they could not hear me.
## Stage 3
- Joreid has taught the wild goats to follow her. Ingrid says they will eat my garden. Ingrid is right. I do not mind.
## Stage 4
- The road up the Tree will open soon, Joreid says. She asked if I would walk her to the bottom of it. I said yes. I will.
## Stage 5
- Joreid went up the Tree with the others, holding my hand to the very first bough. She waved. There are children on the moor now. I tend their gardens too.

# Tor

who: Tor
group: fairies
realm: 3
fairy: 4
kind: crystal pixie
spirits: Kolbein, Lofn
## Backstory
Tor is the oldest-seeming of the Highland fairies, a squat little crystal like a hilltop, slow to speak and never wrong about weather. He
kept the stone on the high pasture above Highmark, where the herders watched the sky. His two are Kolbein, a young dyer who has a strange
thing to say about the one on the high seat, and Lofn, a herder who watched two ravens circle the hills crying the year the gate shut,
after every other raven had left the sky. Tor has watched the sky with Lofn every evening for thirty-six winters, waiting for the ravens to
come back. He believes they will. He is very slow, and very sure.
## Before
- Lofn saw two ravens circle the hills, crying, the year the gate shut. All the others had gone. Those two stayed. She watches for them still.
- Kolbein will tell you something about the one on the high seat. Say nothing back. Remember it.
- I watch the sky with Lofn every evening. Thirty-six winters. No ravens yet. They will come. I am slow but I am not wrong.
- The high pasture was the best grazing in Runeheim. Now it is the best view of the Scar. I would rather have the grass.
- Weather. Tomorrow will be cold, then clear. You may trust me on weather. On everything else, trust Quartz.
## After
- Lofn rose and looked straight up. No ravens. She said, "Soon." I said, "Soon." We have said it every night since. It is a better word than it was.
- Kolbein wants to dye cloth again — grey and gold, he says, for something he has to make. He will not say what.
- I have nothing to watch over now but the sky. It is a big job. I am up to it.
- The pasture is greening. Lofn has four sheep. Sheep do not care about ravens. It is very restful.
- Kolbein said the thing again, about the eye. Then he said, "Your twin will understand it before you do." I hope your twin is clever.
## Life
- They call me Tor because I am shaped like a hilltop. Squat, solid, covered in moss on the north side.
- I am never wrong about weather. I have been wrong about other things. Mostly about people. In their favour, usually.
- I speak slowly. Thinking takes time and I like to do it properly.
- Flint sparks, Aster glows, Quartz shines and Heather blooms. I sit. Somebody has to keep the hill down.
- I have seen every sunset over the Scar for thirty-six winters. Two hundred of them were very good. Eleven were the best things I have ever seen.
## Stage 2
- Rain coming. Good rain. The pasture will thank us.
## Stage 3
- A single raven flew over the high pasture this morning. Not one of the two. Lofn waved at it anyway.
## Stage 4
- Kolbein has finished dyeing his grey and gold cloth. He is sewing it into a cloak. A cloak for someone tall. He says it is "for when he comes home".
## Stage 5
- The two ravens came back. Over the hills, crying — but a glad cry now. Lofn stood and laughed. I was not wrong. I am never wrong about the sky.
