# Asa
key: sp_asa
who: Asa, the weaver of Fernby
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a weaver
## Backstory
An old weaver from Fernby at the crossroads, whose looms made the shawls and blankets for every farm on the Fernby road. She died in the second
winter of the quiet and was bound at the old Fernby stone, where Primrose kept her to a schedule for twenty-eight winters.
## Life
- In Fernby every house had one of my blankets on the best bed. I could tell you whose by the pattern, and how many winters they had slept under it.
- My loom stood by the south window. In the afternoon the light came through the threads and the whole room went gold.
- A weaver counts. Warp, weft, rows, knots. When they chained me, I counted the swings of the pick. It kept me from forgetting how to count anything kinder.
- Primrose made us keep a schedule. Morning was my story. I have told her every story I know at least sixty times. She still laughs at the right places.
- When my line reaches Fernby I want to sit at the crossroads one more afternoon and watch the carts. Just watch them. That is all.

# Bersi
key: sp_bersi
who: Bersi of Fernby
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a shipwright's boy
## Backstory
A boy from Fernby who wanted to build boats and never saw the sea. He died the first winter the gate was shut and waited at the edge of the light
until the Binders took him to a stone; Primrose let him draw his boats in the dirt every noon.
## Life
- I was going to build a ship with a dragon on the front and sail it all the way round the world. I had the dragon drawn already.
- My uncle mended carts in Fernby. He said a cart is just a boat that gave up. I thought that was very funny.
- I drew a boat in the dirt every day at the stone. Primrose counted them. She says it is more boats than the whole Fernheim ever had.
- Being bound was like being stuck in the last minute before supper, forever. Being free is like supper.
- Asa says I can still learn. She says spirits have all the time in the world. I am going to learn knots first. Boats need knots.

# Dagny
key: sp_dagny
who: Dagny, the net-mender of Stonewick
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a net-mender
## Backstory
A young net-mender of Stonewick, the fishing town above Mirror Lake, married to Eyvind the fisher. The Binders chained them at stones a hill
apart, so that each could see the other's light and never reach it.
## Life
- In Stonewick the nets hung on every fence to dry, and in the morning they were silver with dew. The whole hill looked like it was wearing lace.
- I could mend a torn net faster than Eyvind could tear one, and believe me, he tried very hard to tear them.
- For twenty-eight winters I watched his light on the next hill. When it flickered I knew he was tired. When it was bright I knew he was thinking of me.
- The first thing I did when the chain broke was run. I did not even know spirits could run. I ran all the way to his hill.
- The night of the storm I saw two little lights on the water, going home. I think about them often. I hope they got there.

# Eyvind
key: sp_eyvind
who: Eyvind, the fisher of Stonewick
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a fisher
## Backstory
A fisher of Stonewick who rowed out on Mirror Lake every dawn for twenty years, husband to Dagny. He was bound a hill away from her, and spent
the dark years trying to make his light bright enough for her to see.
## Life
- Mirror Lake was so still at dawn that you could not tell where the water stopped and the sky began. I rowed into the sky every morning.
- I tore Dagny's nets on purpose sometimes so she would have something to fuss over. Do not tell her. She knows. She has always known.
- They put me on the next hill from her. I think they meant it as a cruelty. It was the only thing that kept me going.
- Stonewick's boats are all at the bottom of the lake now, I expect. When the line takes us home, I would like to raise one. Just one.
- The lake used to give us fish. You know what it gave us that last night. I am glad it was still generous at the end.

# Frida
key: sp_frida
who: Frida, the beekeeper of Hollowmere
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a beekeeper
## Backstory
A beekeeper in the orchards above Hollowmere, whose straw hives stood between the apple rows. Her bees left the year the sky went quiet; she
followed them in a way, three winters later, and was bound at a stone in her own orchard.
## Life
- Hollowmere honey was the best in Runeheim. Apple blossom and heather. The traders from Fernheim used to come up the road just for it.
- Bees talk to each other with dances. I used to dance back at them. My neighbours thought I was simple. The bees thought I was rude, but kind.
- The bees left in one morning. Every hive. They knew something we did not. I have never been so lonely as that afternoon.
- Clover counted the days for us. She told me once that I had hummed to myself for nine thousand of them. I did not know I hummed.
- If the orchards bloom again, the bees will come back. Bees always come back to good blossom. So will I.

# Geir
key: sp_geir
who: Geir, the carver of Hollowmere
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a carver
## Backstory
An old carver from the sheds of Hollowmere. As a boy, on a summer visit to the last village with his father, he carved the tree into the
Green's stone — the only work of his that still stood when he died.
## Life
- I carved the tree on the Green when I was eleven. My father did the hard parts. I did the roots. I gave it too many roots. Nobody has noticed.
- In Hollowmere we carved everything — doors, spoons, bed-posts, boats for the children of the lake towns. The sheds smelled of pine all year.
- A carver listens to the wood before he cuts it. A bindstone is like that too. Mine whispered all night. Nothing kind.
- I came back to the Green and put my hand on the tree. It was warm. Stone is not supposed to be warm. Someone has been looking after it.
- The roots go further than anyone thinks. I did not carve that on purpose. Or perhaps I did, and only now understand why.

# Hallveig
key: sp_hallveig
who: Hallveig, the cooper
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a cooper
## Backstory
A cooper of the last village — Rolf's wife, and the only one of the forty who lived there. She walked out to the Barrow Downs to fetch staves
in the second year of the quiet and did not come back; Linnet sang to her for twenty-eight winters.
## Life
- I made every barrel in Rolf's cellar. If one leaks, it is not mine. It is from Fernheim. Tell him I said so.
- I went up the Downs road for oak staves. It was only a day's walk. I have been thinking about that day's walk for a long time.
- Linnet sang me the old songs every night. Some of them are about going home. I asked her to stop singing those. She did not stop.
- Rolf still sets two cups out. I saw it from the Green tonight, through the tavern window. I am going to sit by the second one, if he will let me.
- The village is so much louder than I left it. Louder is good. Louder means people.

# Ingolf
key: sp_ingolf
who: Ingolf, the tanner of Stonewick
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a tanner
## Backstory
A young tanner from Stonewick who was chained beside a bindstone the Binders used as a meeting place. He listened, because there was nothing
else to do, and learned more about them than anyone should.
## Life
- Tanning is a smelly trade. In Stonewick they made me work at the bottom of the hill, downwind of everyone. I liked it. Nobody bothered me.
- The Binders met at my stone. They did not care that I could hear. You do not mind what a chained man hears.
- They argue. You would not think it, but they argue like fishwives — about the gold, about who gets which stone, about the one who pays them.
- They do not come from Cinderheim. They come from under it. I have said it before and I will keep saying it until someone writes it down.
- Now that I am free, I want to tan one good hide and make a pair of boots for whoever freed me. Do you know your size?

# Jorunn
key: sp_jorunn
who: Jorunn, the dyer of Fernby
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a dyer
## Backstory
A dyer from the pools by the Fernby water, who could match any blue in the world and whose cloth went to every town of Gildenmark. Sorrel
kept her stone, and the two of them argued about colours for twenty-eight winters.
## Life
- The dye-pools of Fernby were blue, yellow and red, and on a sunny day the water steamed in three colours. Children used to dare each other to touch them.
- Woad for blue, weld for yellow, madder for red. Everything else you make from those three, if you are patient and a little stubborn.
- Every rune in the old days was carved and then painted this blue. My blue. Like your eyes, they say. I do not believe in coincidences.
- Sorrel thinks the sky is grey-blue. It is not. It is cornflower in the morning and lapis at night. We will never agree.
- When I get home I am going to dye one bolt of cloth the colour of the Green tonight, so Gildenmark remember what this looked like.

# Ketil
key: sp_ketil
who: Ketil, the goatherd of Fernby
group: spirits
realm: 1
kind: a goatherd
## Backstory
A goatherd of the Fernby uplands, gruff and quiet, who knew every goat in the hills by name. When he died the goats would not stop crying; he
was bound at the stone by the Fernby water, beside Jorunn.
## Life
- I kept forty goats on the Fernby uplands. Every one had a name. Most of the names were rude, but they were fond names.
- Goats are cleverer than people. A goat would never have let itself be chained to a stone. It would have eaten the chain.
- When I died, the goats cried for three days. I heard them from the edge of the light. Then the chains came and I did not hear anything.
- Up on the uplands you could see four towns' smoke at once — Fernby, Stonewick, Hollowmere and the village. I counted the chimneys every evening.
- When my line reaches the uplands I am going to whistle. If there is a goat left anywhere in Gildenmark, it will come. Goats never forget a whistle.
