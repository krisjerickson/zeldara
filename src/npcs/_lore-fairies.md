# The Keepers of the Stones — the fairies' shared story (round 133; Kris's picks: A as the history, B for each fairy, C woven into the spirits)

The fairies were set to tend the waystones "when the stones were young" — before the villages, before the roads, when the old stone-cutter
laid his hand on the first one and the land answered. Each realm had its five, each fairy one stone, and their work was small and endless:
keep the moss off the runes, sing the stone awake at dusk, and walk the dead to it so they could find the road up the Tree.

When the gate shut, the stones went dark and the road went nowhere. The dead could not go up, and the Binders came for them. The fairies
could not stop the chains — fairy hands are too small for iron — so each did the only thing left: she stayed by the stone nearest to two of
the bound and kept them company. She learned their names, sang their tunes back to them so they would not forget who they had been, and
shrank as the years went on, into a flower, a puddle, a crystal, an ember, until the twin's touch woke the stones again.

So every fairy knows two of the forty spirits better than anyone alive does. Before you free them she tells you who they were; when they
rise she is there; afterwards she still talks about them, and about her own small life — what she kept alive alone, what she misses, what
she is afraid of — and the land healing around her stone.

Where the spirits came from (Kris: "it isn't implied that all of the spirits came from the village … they usually came from a different
quadrant and the lost cities from there"):
- Gildenmark's ten — the outlying farms and hamlets that emptied in the Long Quiet: Fernby, Stonewick, Hollowmere, the Barrow Downs;
  several knew the last village well (Hallveig was Rolf's wife), most never lived in it;
- Fernheim's ten — the Silverrun towns the Black Tide drank: Millbrook, Reedhaven, Hverholm behind its dikes, the Lantern Isles;
- Cragland's ten — the mine-towns and the sea-road: Deepdelve, Kettlecrag, Highmark on the cliff road to the western sea;
- Cinderheim's ten — the great ember-cities of the west, gone in one night: Askvald, Brandhavn, Emberhold under the Ember Wall.
