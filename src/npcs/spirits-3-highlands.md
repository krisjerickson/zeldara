# Arnor
key: sp_arnor
who: Arnor, the weaver of Deepdelve
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a weaver
## Backstory
A weaver of Deepdelve, the deepest mine-town in Runeheim, who wove the miners' heavy cloth. He heard the stone singing in the year before the
Stone-Fall, as the miners did, and like them did not listen to the fairy who told them not to follow it.
## Life
- Miners' cloth has to be thick enough to keep out the cold of the deep and soft enough to sleep in on a ledge. I was the best at it.
- In Deepdelve you lived half your life underground. The lamps never went out. We called the sun "the big lamp" and some of us forgot what it looked like.
- The stone sang that last year. Low and sweet, from very far down. The miners dug toward it. I wove faster, as if that would help.
- Flint told us not to follow the song. She was right. I have told her so every day since. She says it gets less satisfying each time.
- I want to weave a blanket the colour of daylight. I have nearly forgotten it, and I would like to have something to remember it by.

# Bodil
key: sp_bodil
who: Bodil, the shipwright of Highmark
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a shipwright
## Backstory
An old shipwright of Highmark, the last town on the cliff road to the western sea, who built ships high in the mountains to be carried down to
water no one had sailed in generations. The Great Scar took the road to the sea.
## Life
- People laughed at a shipyard on a mountain. Then they saw the ships. Then they stopped laughing and started asking how we would get them down.
- From the Highmark peaks you could see the western sea on a clear day, flat and silver. We meant to sail it to the edge of the world.
- The Scar split the road in a single night. Our best ship was halfway down the cliff on ropes. It is still there, I think, hanging.
- Flint keeps telling me Craglanders are not sailors. I tell her Craglanders are whatever they decide to be. She sparks at me. I like her.
- If the road to the sea opens again, I would like to finish that ship. She has waited long enough. So have I.

# Dalla
key: sp_dalla
who: Dalla, the net-mender of Kettlecrag
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a net-mender
## Backstory
A net-mender of Kettlecrag, where the mountain lakes are so clear you can see to the bottom. Chained near the deep places, she learned of a
chamber under the mountains where light is kept chained like hounds.
## Life
- Kettlecrag nets are fine as hair. The lake fish are small and clever and see a clumsy knot from three fathoms away.
- I mended nets on the jetty every afternoon. The water was so still you could mend by the reflection of your own hands.
- There is a chamber under the mountains where light is chained like hounds. I heard it straining. Have you found it yet? Be careful when you do.
- Aster sat in my lap for twenty-eight winters, glowing a little, so I was never quite in the dark. I do not know how to thank a star.
- When the lakes are fished again I will mend every net on that jetty for nothing. Just to feel the knots again.

# Einar
key: sp_einar
who: Einar, the fisher of Kettlecrag
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a fisher
## Backstory
A young fisher of Kettlecrag who rowed out on the mountain lakes at night, because on still nights you could see the great white city reflected
in the water. One night all he saw there was a golden mask.
## Life
- On still nights, the lake showed you a city of white towers that was not there when you looked up. Every fisher in Kettlecrag had seen it.
- I used to row out to the middle and lie in the boat and look down into it instead of up. People thought I was fishing. I was dreaming.
- One night the city was gone from the water. Only a golden face, very still, looking up at me. I rowed home and never fished at night again.
- Aster says the city is still there. She says reflections do not lie, they only wait. I want to believe her.
- I want to row out one more night when the lake is calm. Just to see what the water shows me now.

# Gudrun
key: sp_gudrun
who: Gudrun, the beekeeper of Highmark
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a beekeeper
## Backstory
A beekeeper of Highmark, whose hives stood on the heather slopes below the cliff road. Her queen bee was golden, and the Binders crushed her
first, before they came for the keeper.
## Life
- Cragland heather honey is dark as ale and you could taste the whole mountain in it. Frida in Hollowmere and I traded honey every summer by cart.
- My queen was golden. Not yellow — golden, like a coin. The hive sang around her. I have never heard anything like it since.
- The Binders crushed her first. They knew. A hive without its queen goes quiet. So did Highmark, soon after.
- Quartz says I talk to the bees as if they could answer. They do answer. You just have to be very patient and very still.
- I want to find a wild hive on the heather and sit by it. I will not take any honey. I will just listen.

# Hakon
key: sp_hakon
who: Hakon, the carver of Highmark
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a carver
## Backstory
A rune-carver of Highmark who noticed, carving the old mountain stones, that every rune in Cragland was carved pointing toward the lake —
as if it were the middle of everything.
## Life
- I carved runes for doorposts, graves, bridges, boundary stones. Cragland granite is hard, and a rune in it lasts a thousand years.
- Every old rune in the mountains points toward the lake. Every one. I measured them with string. Nobody wanted to hear it.
- A rune is a word that does not need a mouth. That is why the Binders hate them, I think. You cannot chain a word.
- Quartz helped me count the runes. You can see the sun through her. It made the counting easier.
- When I am home I am going to carve one new rune. A small one. On a stone by the lake. Pointing outward, for once.

# Ingrid
key: sp_ingrid
who: Ingrid, the cooper of Kettlecrag
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a cooper
## Backstory
An old cooper of Kettlecrag, buried on the high moor where the Kettlecrag families laid their own. She saw the Great Scar split three winters
after the gate shut, and believes it was the mountain answering the fire below.
## Life
- A good barrel holds anything: water, ale, salt fish, a family's whole winter. I made barrels that outlived three generations of owners.
- Three winters after the gate shut, the ground cracked from the lakes to the sea in one night. The Scar. I felt it in my teeth.
- I think it was the mountain answering. Something burned below it, far away south. The mountain cracked rather than give in.
- Heather sits with me and Joreid on the moor. She is soft-hearted, Heather. She cries over everything. I let her. Someone has to.
- Hallveig, the cooper in Gildenmark, and I wrote to each other about staves for thirty years. I would like to meet her at last.

# Joreid
key: sp_joreid
who: Joreid of Kettlecrag
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a little one of the high moor
## Backstory
The last little one born in Cragland, who lived three summers on the high moor above Kettlecrag. Heather has loved Joreid like her own for
twenty-eight winters.
## Life
- I like the purple flowers best. Heather says they are her. I say they are mine. We share.
- Ingrid made me a little barrel to sit in. I sat in it every day. It is the best chair in Cragland.
- I don't remember much. I remember the lake was cold and Mother's hands were warm. That is enough to remember.
- The chains were heavy, so Heather sat on them. She is very small but she is very stubborn. They were lighter after.
- Now that I am free I am going to roll down the whole moor. Ingrid says it is undignified. Ingrid is old.

# Kolbein
key: sp_kolbein
who: Kolbein, the dyer of Highmark
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a dyer
## Backstory
A young dyer of Highmark whose purples and greens dressed the cliff town. Chained on the high pasture, he has a strange thing to say about the
one on the high seat, and Tor has heard him say it four thousand times.
## Life
- Highmark purple came from a lichen on the north cliffs. You scraped it at dawn, with your back to the wind, or you scraped your own fingers.
- I was going to be the best dyer in Runeheim. Then I heard about Jorunn of Fernby and her blue, and I was going to be the second best.
- The one on the high seat has one eye too. But his eye has never seen anything. I said it once and they chained me further from the path.
- Tor never says much. When I said it for the four-thousandth time he said, "Yes." It was the best conversation I had in twenty-eight winters.
- I would like to meet Jorunn and argue about blue. I have prepared my arguments. Twenty-eight winters' worth.

# Lofn
key: sp_lofn
who: Lofn, the herder of Highmark
group: spirits
realm: 3
kind: a herder
## Backstory
A herder of the high pasture above Highmark, who watched the sky for a living. The year the gate shut she saw two ravens circle the hills, crying,
after every other raven had gone.
## Life
- A herder watches the sky more than the herd. Weather, birds, smoke. The herd mostly watches itself.
- After the gate shut, every raven left. All but two. They circled our hills for a week, crying. Then they were gone too.
- I do not know where they went. I have looked at every bird since, just in case.
- Tor can tell the weather three days off by the ache in his crystal. He has been right four thousand times. I have counted.
- When my line takes me home I will climb to the high pasture and look for two black birds. If I see them, I will wave.
