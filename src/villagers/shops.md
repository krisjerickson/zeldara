# Benno the Baker
key: benno
who: Benno the Baker
meets: 44
age: 57
home: the bakery-merchant on the market square
pick: Relentlessly cheery pun-maker
voice: Bright, quick and punning; the cheer is real, and it is also how he carries things he will not say.

## Backstory
Benno was born above the bakery, the son of Yrsa, a Wetlands baker who married into the village and never stopped talking about the marsh. Every midsummer Yrsa baked a great braided loaf "for the feast in Asgard" and set it on the Runestone for the gods to take up. The last year anybody remembers, no one came for it. That was the year Benno turned twenty-one, the night the sky went red.

The day before the Ash Night, Yrsa had started a new sourdough and laughed that it would "outlive them all". Benno named it Old Mother. Eight years later Yrsa went home to the Wetlands to bury her sister, and the Black Tide took the marsh villages while she was there. She never came back. Benno has fed Old Mother every morning for twenty-eight years, because it is the one thing of hers that is still alive.

He took over the shop, then the merchant's trade when the old merchant died in the Long Quiet, because someone had to buy what the few brave hunters brought home. He decided early that a village without children needed somebody to be silly in it, and he has been aggressively, exhaustingly cheerful ever since. He has a shelf of honey-cake moulds — stars, foxes, little boats — that have never been used. He dusts them on Sundays. He tells everyone it is for the shape of the shelf.

## In the Saga
- The merchant: buys your loot after almost every trip home; sells bread and food.
- Old Mother's Rescue: marsh rye, snow-honey and ember-salt to save the starter.
- His mother Yrsa is one of the forty bound spirits (the Wetlands baker: "Bread for a feast in Asgard, every midsummer. The last year, no one came to take it up.").
- After the end he bakes the first honey cakes in twenty-eight years, for the first newborns.

## Ties
- Morwen: rivals — "your potions are liquid bread"; secretly each other's best customer.
- Tilda: the eternal argument over who is the village's real merchant.
- Oswin: Yrsa baked the Ravens' road-bread; Benno still bakes the same loaf for him.
- Rolf: supplies the tavern's bread and argues about the crust.

# Morwen the Sorcerer
key: morwen
who: Morwen the Sorcerer
meets: 40
age: 61
home: the apothecary
pick: The Serkland alchemist
voice: Grand, precise and theatrical; every potion is a poem and every sale a lecture with footnotes. Warmer than she lets on, and haunted by one book.

## Backstory
Morwen was born in a harbour town in the far north of Runeheim and went south at seventeen as a clerk on a trading ship. In Serkland, beyond the sea, she found the man who became her teacher: Zahir, called the Silver-Tongued, an alchemist and trader in rare things who could sell salt to the sea. He taught her distillation, the poetry of measures and the habit of footnotes. Forty years ago she came north with him on his last great caravan, guarded by a young Varangian named Brask.

Zahir traded in more than herbs. In his locked books Morwen once read a song — a binding-song, a verse for chaining a thing that wants to rise. He told her it was "only an old curiosity" and sold it, she believes, to buyers who paid in strange red gold. Four years later the gate shut, the Ash Night came, and Zahir sailed south with the last ship before the walls went up. Morwen stayed. She has never been certain whether she stayed to help, or to hide from what she had read.

She opened the apothecary, became the village's only healer of note and its most exhausting conversationalist, and made a vow never to sell a draught she could not explain. In the back room she keeps Zahir's three remaining books, wrapped in oilcloth. She has not opened the third since the Ash Night.

## In the Saga
- The apothecary: potions after most trips home; sells the Sovereign staff, a Saga step.
- Ingredients of Doom and the Spoon riddle (her side quests).
- Her arc: the grand alchemist → her teacher from Serkland → the binding-song she once read → the Counter-Verse, a draught that loosens a chain "the way vinegar loosens rust" → after the end, nothing left to explain, so she explains anyway.
- One spirit's memory ("The Binders paid in Muspelheim gold. Some of us took it.") is the shadow over her teacher.

## Ties
- Brask: guarded Zahir's caravan; the two strangers who stayed. They play chess on Fridays and both cheat.
- Benno: the bread-and-potion rivalry; she secretly buys his rye for her tinctures.
- Sister Mael: the only other healer in Runeheim; they write each other long, rude, admiring letters about each other's methods.
- Tilda: Tilda's mystery key fits a lock Morwen has seen before.

# Tilda the Merchant
key: tilda
who: Tilda the Merchant
meets: 28
age: 66
home: the general shop
pick: The cheerfully rude shopkeeper
voice: Blunt, quick and insulting in the friendliest possible way; she has never said 'please' and has never short-changed anyone.

## Backstory
Tilda took over the general shop from her father at nineteen and has run it with an iron ledger and an iron tongue ever since. She calls every customer "you" — not "dear", not "friend", just "you" — and has been known to refuse to sell a rope to someone because "you'll hang yourself with it, you idiot". She is, everyone agrees, the most honest person in the village. Her prices are fair. Her opinions are not.

Twenty-eight years ago, on the evening of the Ash Night, a tall Ashlander in a grey cloak bought three days' supplies on credit and left her an iron key as surety: "I'll be back for it in a week." The Ember Wall rose that night. He never came back. Tilda has kept the key on a nail behind the counter all this time, partly out of principle — he still owes her eleven silver — and partly because she cannot bring herself to throw away the last thing an Ashlander ever gave anyone.

She never married. "Never met a man who could count." She keeps a list in the back of her ledger titled REGULARS. It has been empty for twenty years, since the last old trapper died.

## In the Saga
- The general shop: supplies and ammunition all game.
- Her arc: rude to you → grudging respect when you bring something rare → the merchants' rivalry with Benno → the mystery key opens something in the Ashlands (side quest) → after the end she writes your name on the REGULARS list, her highest honour.

## Ties
- Benno: who is the village's best merchant? (She is. Ask her.)
- Morwen: the only villager who can out-talk her; Tilda pretends to hate her and lends her the good scales.
- Corr: buys ink from her for his poems; she charges him extra "for the damage".
- Pell: they disagree about everything, especially Pell.

# Garrick the Blacksmith
key: garrick
who: Garrick the Blacksmith
meets: 26
age: 59
home: the forge
pick: The forgery hunter
voice: Gruff, few words, all of them about metal. Can tell bad steel by its ring and a lie by its pause. One rule: no chains.

## Backstory
Garrick was born in the Highlands, in a forge town on the lip of the deep mines, and learned the trade from his elder sister Thora, the best smith in three valleys. When Garrick was twenty-three, Thora married an Ashlander and moved west. A year later the Ash Night came. Her last letter, sent before the wall rose, said that strangers with red gold had come to the Ashland forges wanting chains — "heavy ones, too heavy for any beast". She had refused. Then the letters stopped.

Three winters on, the Highland mines dug deeper after "a voice in the stone", and the mountains broke. Garrick's wife Ingrid, a cooper, died in the Stone-Fall. He walked out of the Highlands with his hammer and his anvil-horn and nothing else, and came to the last village, where the old smith had just died.

He hunts forgeries the way other men hunt deer: fake Highland steel, false gold, swords that are paint over pig-iron. He says a lie in metal is the worst kind of lie, because a person trusts their life to it. And he will not forge a chain — not a dog's chain, not a well chain, not a chain for a gate. He has never explained why. Thora is one of the forty spirits, bound in the Ashlands: "We forged the Binders' chains ourselves. They made us. Then they put them on us."

## In the Saga
- The forge: upgrades and crafting all game; the Coal side quest ("six lumps — honest ones").
- His arc: grump → Dunn is freed and it's dwarf against human pride (the forge-off) → they team up on your best weapon → after the end he beats a sword into a ploughshare for the new hamlets. Throughout: no chains.
- His sister Thora (an Ashlands spirit) and wife Ingrid (a Highlands spirit) are among the forty.

## Ties
- Dunn: rival, then partner; they agree on nothing except steel.
- Brask: Garrick made the Varangian's axe over again from the broken old one; Brask still thinks it's the original.
- Bram: argues about rivets.
- Mira: she uses his scrap; he pretends not to know.

# Sergeant Brask
key: brask
who: Sergeant Brask
meets: 18
age: 63
home: the armoury
pick: The homesick Varangian
voice: Formal, booming and sentimental; salutes everyone, compares everything to Miklagard (unfavourably), and sings in his mother tongue while he polishes.

## Backstory
Brask was born in the cold north of a far country and went south as a young man to join the Varangian guard of the Emperor in Miklagard, the Great City, where the domes are gold and the bread is better. At twenty-three he took leave to guard a caravan north — Zahir the Silver-Tongued, his young apprentice Morwen, and forty mules of rare goods — "a three-month job". It was forty years ago.

Four years after he arrived, the realms closed. The ships stopped. Brask stayed, because Oswin needed someone who knew which end of a spear went where, and because the village needed a wall-guard who could stand still for nine hours. He became the armourer and the village's sergeant of a militia that has never had more than six members. He still keeps his Varangian guard's red cloak folded in a cedar chest, and once a year he puts it on and salutes south.

He writes a letter to the Emperor's guard every winter, reporting on the village's defences in loving detail. He has never been able to send one. There are thirty-five of them in the chest. He is not sure the Emperor he served is still alive. He is sure the bread is still better.

## In the Saga
- The armoury: armour all game.
- A Letter to Miklagard: give his letter to Captain Vela, whose ships go south "with the next good wind".
- After the end, with the sea roads open, he must choose: go home, or stay home.

## Ties
- Morwen: the caravan they came on; chess on Fridays; both cheat.
- Oswin: salutes him though he was never his captain.
- Vela: the only person who can actually carry his letters.
- Corr: Brask trained him; despairs of his poems.

# Rolf the Bartender
key: rolf
who: Rolf the Bartender
meets: 12
age: 55
home: the tavern
pick: The flyting champion
voice: Loud, rhythmic and quick; insults in verse, with alliteration, for fun — and a quiet man the moment the tavern empties.

## Backstory
Rolf is the undefeated flyting champion of the last village — a flyting being a contest of insults in verse, traded across a table until one poet runs out of words. He has been undefeated for twenty-eight years. Before that, he lost exactly once, to a cooper named Hallveig, who called him "a beer-barrel with a beard, bung-holed and bubbling" and then married him.

They ran the tavern together for ten years. Hallveig died in the eighth winter of the Long Quiet, of a fever Morwen could not break. With the gate shut she did not go anywhere; she drifted, as the Grasslands dead did, at the edge of the light, and then the Binders came over the walls for her too. Rolf does not know that last part. He only knows that every night, after closing, he sets two cups on the bar and fills them both.

He brews Skald's Mead from honey, berries and salt, and claims it is the mead of poetry itself. He keeps Oswin's old Raven mug behind the bar and lets no one else drink from it. He has never let a stranger see him sad, and has never once, in twenty-eight years, flyted someone who was already down.

## In the Saga
- The tavern: meals, drink and rest.
- The Mead side quest (honey, berries, salt — "even a troll could remember that").
- His wife Hallveig is one of the forty spirits (the Grasslands cooper: "My husband still sets two cups out. Tell him I drink the light now.").

## Ties
- Oswin: his mug; the one flyting that took two hours and a stool.
- Benno: bread for the tavern, and a running feud about the crust.
- Corr: Rolf reads Corr's poems aloud at the bar, with feeling, to Corr's horror.
- Tob: Tob's turnip Sigurd has a permanent seat at the end of the bar.

# Iva the Jeweler
key: iva
who: Iva the Jeweler
meets: 11
age: 52
home: the jeweller's
pick: The gem whisperer
voice: Soft, precise and a little dreamy; talks to stones as if they were shy animals, and hears more than she says.

## Backstory
Iva's mother Jorunn was a dyer who could match any blue in Runeheim, and Iva grew up among vats of woad and cloth the colour of the old rune-light. She found she could do the same with stones: hold a gem up to her ear, she says, and it tells you where it was cut and whether it was cut kindly. People laugh. Then she tells them which mine their grandmother's ring came from, and they stop laughing.

When Iva was sixteen the gate shut, and something changed in the stones. Gems went dull all over Runeheim. Rings that had glittered for generations looked like river pebbles. Her mother said even the blue had gone out of the dye. Jorunn died in the Long Quiet, and Iva kept the shop open with whatever came in — mostly mourning rings, mostly dull.

Then, sixteen years ago, she held a dull sapphire near a cradle in Runa's house and it flickered. She has never told anyone this, because she has no idea what it means. She keeps that sapphire in a box lined with her mother's last blue cloth. When the twin you play comes in for the first time, every stone in the shop wakes up.

## In the Saga
- The jeweller: gems and rings.
- The Hoard riddle: "Where the river-wall broke, beside the first stone, look under the moss."
- Gems and runestones light for the twins — she sees it more clearly than anyone, and keeps the secret that she doesn't understand.
- Her mother Jorunn is one of the forty spirits (the Grasslands dyer: "Blue. Every rune in the old days was this blue. Like your eyes, they say.").

## Ties
- Coral: sells her "beach silver"; Iva knows exactly which beaches.
- Pell: they argue about colours with real passion.
- Oswin: told him his old ring is glass — "sentimental glass".
- Sólveig: the two of them can sit in silence for an hour, listening to glass and stone.

# Hana the Stable Master
key: hana
who: Hana the Stable Master
meets: 8
age: 70
home: the stables
pick: Speaks through the horse
voice: Says what she thinks by saying what the horse thinks. "Bramble says you smell of trolls." Bramble is never wrong.

## Backstory
Hana and her brother Ketil grew up herding goats and horses in the hills above the village, and nobody ever saw one without the other. Ketil talked to the goats; Hana talked to the horses; neither of them talked much to people. When their parents died they took over the village stables and the hill herds together.

In the second winter of the Quiet, Ketil died of a fall in the hills. The goats, Hana says, would not stop crying for a week. Then, fourteen winters later, two babes came out of Mirror Lake, and the village had no mothers and no milk. Hana walked up the hill, milked Ketil's goats, and fed them both, every two hours, for a year. She has never said one sentimental word about it. "The goats did it. I held the bucket."

These days she keeps a few mounts and a great many opinions, all of which she attributes to Bramble, a bad-tempered old grey mare with a long memory. She remembers the Ravens' four riderless horses coming home from the Emberford, and so, she says, does Bramble. Ketil is one of the forty spirits: "When I died the goats wouldn't stop crying. Funny — goats raised you."

## In the Saga
- The stables: horses and mounts.
- The Foal side quest.
- Raised the twins on goat's milk, which "put the light out — or hid it."

## Ties
- Oswin: her horses have never forgiven him the Ash Night; neither has Bramble.
- Runa: the two oldest women in the village; they share a bench and silence.
- Tob: his crows bother her horses; his turnips are acceptable.
- The twins: she calls you both "kid". It is the most affectionate word she owns.

# Pell the Tailor
key: pell
who: Pell the Tailor
meets: 6
age: 48
home: the tailor's
pick: The fashion snob
voice: Withering, theatrical and exact; judges every outfit in the village and is right about all of them. Sighs as punctuation.

## Backstory
Pell was twelve when old Asa the weaver took him in as an apprentice, the only boy in the village who wanted to learn. Asa wove the finest cloth in the Grasslands, and she was weaving birth-shawls — little white ones with blue runes along the hem — the year the gate shut. She kept weaving them. Twenty of them, for children who never came. She was buried in one.

Pell inherited her loom, her shears, her eye and none of her patience. He decided that if Runeheim had to be grey and grieving, it would at least be well-dressed, and he has spent thirty years trying to stop the village from looking like a pile of wet sacks. He fails. He keeps trying. He has opinions about Oswin's cloak (a tragedy), Brask's red one (magnificent, wasted in a chest), and the hero's moss-green cape (promising; badly hemmed).

He talks about Miklagard silk the way other men talk about first love. He has never touched any. He kept one of Asa's twenty shawls, folded in tissue, and has never shown it to anyone. Asa is one of the forty spirits: "I wove twenty birth-shawls that no child ever wore. I was buried in one."

## In the Saga
- The tailor: clothing.
- The Skysilk side quest (and the silk cape).

## Ties
- Brask: wants that red cloak out of the chest and onto Brask.
- Iva: colour arguments of real ferocity.
- Tilda: they disagree about everything, especially Tilda.
- Vela: the only person in Runeheim whose coat he approves of.
