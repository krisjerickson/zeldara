# Your twin
key: twin
who: your twin
meets: 30
age: about 16
home: the Home, and the library by day
pick: The know-it-all sibling
voice: Teasing, quick and keeping score; calls you "the one who hits things". Under the jokes, the most careful mind in the village — and frightened for you in every letter.

## Backstory
Your twin came out of Mirror Lake in the same basket as you, sixteen winters ago, with the same rune-blue eyes and the same appetite for Hana's goat's milk. From the start you were opposites who refused to be apart: you climbed the Runestone; they read what was carved on it. By six they could read runes no one else in the village could; by ten they were correcting Elder Runa's sagas, out loud, at dinner.

When the village chose who would go out into Runeheim, it was never really a choice. You were the one who could swing a sword; they were the one who could read the oldest books — the ones in no living tongue that came, Runa says, "from before the road was shut". They stay in the library by day and the Home by night, keeping the village going and building the string wall: a wall of maps, pins and red thread where every rumour, every freed spirit's memory and every line of light you report gets its place.

They keep score of everything — monsters, spirits, who's ahead. They are ahead. They are also, though they would never say it to your face, the one person in the village who lies awake when you are out past dark, and who reads the sagas faster every time you leave.

## In the Saga
- Talks in the library as the story moves, mumbles at night, and pieces it together: "The line it left — I've seen that pattern before…"
- At twenty freed: "Bring me the map. The lines… they're drawing something."
- Finds GODSBANE in the oldest saga, teaches it to you, comes to Asgard and casts it with you in the feast hall.
- Their arc: teasing and keeping score → the string wall and the first wrong-but-funny theory → deductions that get scary-accurate → worried, brave letters → after the end they write the saga of you both and give you top billing, grudgingly.

## Ties
- Elder Runa: teacher, sparring partner, and the only person who can still surprise them.
- Oswin: took three hours of notes on the Ravens; Oswin has never been so flattered.
- Morwen: borrows her footnotes; she borrows their sagas.
- Vigdis the skald (a Wetlands spirit) wrote the saga with the missing verse that the twin has been chasing for years.

# Elder Runa
key: runa
who: Elder Runa
meets: 10
age: 88
home: the Runestone Green
pick: The mischievous grandmother
voice: Sly, warm and half-remembered; quotes her grandmother's grandmother, cheats at hnefatafl, and is far sharper than she pretends.

## Backstory
Runa is the oldest person in the last village and the keeper of the Runestone, the old stone on the Green with the World Tree carved into it. She learned the old lore at her grandmother's knee — the cycle of souls, the road the dead walk home, the gods at the top of the tree — and she has been teaching it, garbled and glorious, to anyone who would sit still for eighty years.

Her husband Geir carved the Green's tree into the Runestone when he was a boy, apprenticed to the old carvers; he used to say the roots went further than anyone thought. Geir died in the eleventh winter of the Quiet. Runa sat by the stone the next spring and noticed what nobody else wanted to notice: the tree on the Green had gone dim. The road was shut. The dead were not going home.

For twelve years she said nothing about it, because a village with no children did not need to hear that its dead were stuck too. Then the storm came, the lightning walked from runestone to runestone across the Grasslands, and in the morning Oswin carried a basket up from the pier and put two glowing babes in her lap. She says that was the day she started cheating at hnefatafl again. "Hope makes you playful."

## In the Saga
- The first walk ends with her: the waystone lesson.
- Old lore through the whole game: "The tree on the Green is the road the dead walk home. Lately… it has been quiet."
- Freed spirits gather on her Green in rings; she greets every one by name if she knew them.
- Her husband Geir is one of the forty spirits (the Grasslands carver: "I carved the Green's tree when I was a boy. The roots go further than anyone thinks.").
- Hnefatafl against Runa (a mini-game Kris picked).

## Ties
- Oswin: has beaten him at hnefatafl every year since they were nine.
- Hana: they share a bench and a silence.
- Your twin: the student who overtook her, which delights her.
- Old Nell: the three of them — Runa, Nell and Oswin — were on the pier the dawn the basket came.

# Old Nell the Fisher
key: nell
who: Old Nell the Fisher
meets: 7
age: 79
home: the pier
pick: The tall-tale fisher
voice: Salty, sly and enormous in her exaggerations — every pike is the size of a boat — except for one story, which she tells small and true.

## Backstory
Nell has fished Mirror Lake for sixty years from the end of the pier, and in that time she has caught, by her own account, a pike the size of a longship, an eel that could whistle, and a trout that bit Oswin. Nobody believes a word. She and her husband Eyvind and her sister Dagny worked the lake together — Eyvind on the water, Dagny mending the nets on the pier, Nell telling everyone how it went.

The lake went quiet with the gate. The fish thinned. Eyvind died in the fifth winter of the Quiet and Dagny two winters later, and Nell fished on alone with the net Dagny had mended for her, because "a net's a net and a lake's a lake".

Sixteen years ago, at dawn after the great storm, that net came up heavy. In it, tangled and floating, was a wicker basket, and in the basket two babes with eyes that glowed rune-blue. It is the one story Nell tells small. She says: "My net brought them in. That's all." She lost the net to the far shore this past year — the bones walk there at dusk — and when you bring it back, it is not the gold she is grateful for.

## In the Saga
- Old Nell's net: one of Oswin's first three tasks; she pays fifty gold and says "Oswin sent a good one."
- The Ox-head side quest (the skull for the lake).
- Her husband Eyvind and sister Dagny are both among the forty spirits ("The lake used to give us fish. That night it gave you." / "The night of the storm I saw it from my stone: two little lights on the water, going home.").

## Ties
- Oswin: on the pier together the dawn the basket came.
- Hesk: builds her boats and argues about the singing.
- Ilsa: after the end, the two of them watch the lake from either end of the harbour.

# Watchman Corr
key: corr
who: Watchman Corr
meets: 3
age: 44
home: the village gate
pick: Writes awful love poems
voice: Earnest, hopeful and terrible at rhyme; reads his poems aloud to anyone who stands still at the gate.

## Backstory
Corr was eight when the Ash Night came, a boy from the Ashlands' eastern edge visiting the last village with his big brother Njal for the summer fair. Njal went home ahead of him to fetch the cart. The Ember Wall rose that night, and Corr was on one side of it and his brother on the other. He has lived in the village ever since, raised by whoever had a spare bed, which was eventually Brask.

He joined the gate watch at sixteen because, he says, he wanted to be standing at the gate when somebody came back. Nobody ever did. So he started writing poems to pass the nights — love poems, all of them, to the light on the point: Ilsa, the lighthouse keeper, who has lit her lamp every night of the Quiet for ships that never came. He has watched that light from the gate for twenty-eight years. The poems are very bad. "O Ilsa, light of the lighthouse, you are brighter than a… fight mouse." He has three hundred of them.

He has never once left his post during a watch. He has never once climbed the hill to the lighthouse.

## In the Saga
- Lines at the gate.
- The Poem side quest.
- His brother Njal is one of the forty spirits (the Ashlands stonemason: "The Ember Wall rose in one night. I was on the wrong side of it.").

## Ties
- Ilsa: the poems' entire subject; A Poem for Ilsa is his side quest.
- Brask: took him in; drilled him; cannot bear the poems.
- Rolf: reads them aloud at the tavern. Corr has asked him to stop. Corr has not asked very firmly.
- Tilda: sells him ink "at the poetry rate".

# Marta the Cart Vendor
key: marta
who: Marta the Cart Vendor
meets: 3
age: 61
home: the market carts (from the Highlands on)
pick: The river trader
voice: Brisk, salty and nostalgic; talks prices and currents in the same breath, and judges vegetables like a river pilot judges water.

## Backstory
Before the Black Tide, Marta and her husband Magni ran a flat-bottomed trading boat up and down the Silverrun, from the Wetland mills to the Grassland fords — cheese up, eels down, gossip both ways. Magni was a stonemason by trade who built dikes in the off-season, and every year he built them higher.

In the eighth winter of the Quiet the Silverrun rose black from the south. "The water didn't come over the dikes," Magni had told her once, laughing, "if it ever does, it'll come up through." It came up through. Marta was on the boat at the Grassland ford; Magni was on the dike. The marshes drank the villages in a night, and the river became a wall.

She sold the boat, bought a cart, and has spent twenty-eight years selling vegetables in the last village — "a river trader with no river". She comes to the market square once the roads open enough to bring her goods, and she talks about the Silverrun as if it were a person who left without saying goodbye.

## In the Saga
- Lines at the market carts (from the Highlands on).
- Her husband Magni is one of the forty spirits (the Wetlands stonemason: "We built the dikes higher every year. The water didn't come over them. It came up through.").
- When Bram's bridge rises, she is the first across with a cart.

## Ties
- Tob: buys his vegetables, refuses to learn their names.
- Bram: worked with Magni once; she thinks Bram's bridge is "adequate".
- Oswin: once sailed with Magni and Ulf with a hold full of cheese; she tells it differently.

# Tob the Farmer
key: tob
who: Tob the Farmer
meets: 2
age: 67
home: the fields
pick: Names his vegetables
voice: Gentle, slow and devoted; introduces his turnips by name and grieves at harvest.

## Backstory
Tob has farmed the same three fields beside the village for fifty years, and for the last twenty-eight of them he has named every vegetable he grows. It started the year the gate shut, when his wife Frida's bees left the hives all at once and the fields went quiet. Tob found that talking to the cabbages helped. Then naming them helped more.

Frida kept bees at the edge of the fields. She always said the bees knew things before people did. When they left that spring she cried for a week, and she was never quite well after; she died in the ninth winter of the Quiet. Tob kept her hives clean and empty for twenty-seven years, in case the bees came back.

His most famous vegetable is Sigurd, a turnip of great size and dignity who has a permanent seat at the end of Rolf's bar. Tob does not eat the ones he names. He sells them to Marta, who eats them, and they have agreed never to discuss it.

## In the Saga
- Lines in the fields.
- The Crows side quest.
- His wife Frida is one of the forty spirits (the Grasslands beekeeper: "The bees left the year the sky went quiet. They knew before we did.").
- After the end — if the bees come back — he names every one of them.

## Ties
- Rolf: Sigurd's seat at the bar.
- Hana: his crows bother her horses.
- Marta: buys his vegetables; they do not discuss it.

# Greta the Miller
key: greta
who: Greta the Miller
meets: 2
age: 58
home: the windmill
pick: The wind superstitions
voice: Watchful and superstitious; reads every gust, spits to the west, and is never wrong about the weather, which only makes it worse.

## Backstory
Greta was born in a Wetlands mill-town on the Silverrun, the elder of two miller's daughters. Her little sister Liv took over the family water-mill; Greta, who could not stand the damp, went north to run the last village's windmill. "Water mills are for people who trust rivers."

She was right. In the eighth winter of the Quiet the Silverrun ran black from the south and Liv's mill-wheel, by the account of the one cart that escaped, turned backwards before the water took it. Greta has trusted nothing since but the wind, and she trusts the wind with a passion. A north wind means news. An east wind means guests. A west wind — from the Ashlands — means trouble, and she spits at it.

She judges the whole village by which way its chimneys smoke, and she has never once been caught in the rain.

## In the Saga
- Lines at the windmill.
- The Wheel riddle.
- Her sister Liv is one of the forty spirits (the Wetlands miller: "The Silverrun ran black from the south, and the mill wheel turned backwards.").

## Ties
- Benno: buys all her flour, and asks every morning which way the wind is blowing for the bread.
- Hesk: argues that planks don't sing, wind does.
- Oswin: spits west when he passes; says he brings a bad wind.

# Hesk the Boatwright
key: hesk
who: Hesk the Boatwright
meets: 2
age: 72
home: the boathouse
pick: Hears the planks sing
voice: Dreamy, patient and certain; hears a note in every plank and will not launch a boat that sings off-key.

## Backstory
Hesk and his sister Bodil learned boatbuilding from their father in the Highlands, in a valley where shipwrights built boats for a sea they could only see from the peaks. Bodil believed one day they would sail to the end of the sea and up into the sky — "to sail to the gods". Hesk only wanted to build a boat that sang right.

He came down to the lake to work the year before the gate shut. Bodil stayed in the Highlands, and the Stone-Fall took her and the road to the sea together. Hesk has built every boat on Mirror Lake for forty years, and he says every plank has a note: oak hums low, pine sings high, and a good hull is a chord. He will tear down a boat that sings off-key, which has made him poor and very well respected.

Since the gate shut, he says, the lake has been out of tune. He has been waiting twenty-eight years for it to come right.

## In the Saga
- Lines at the boathouse.
- Oswin's Proper Paperwork: the "sea-steed's keeper" in the kenning is Hesk.
- His sister Bodil is one of the forty spirits (the Highlands shipwright: "We built ships for a sea we could see from the peaks, to sail to the gods.").
- When Asgard comes down onto the lake, he hears it in tune for the first time in twenty-eight years.

## Ties
- Nell: builds her boats; she says they sing like gulls.
- Greta: wind against planks — the oldest argument in the village.
- Quill: buys his boats for the harbour islands and haggles in a way Hesk finds musically unpleasant.

# Old Fenwick
key: fenwick
who: Old Fenwick
meets: 2
age: 81
home: the lamp-posts, at dusk (from the Ashlands on)
pick: Dark and dismal
voice: Slow, gloomy and grimly funny; expects the worst, describes it beautifully, and lights the lamps anyway.

## Backstory
Fenwick has lit the village's lamps every dusk for sixty years. He was a cheerful man once, people say, though nobody can remember it clearly. He lost his wife to the Long Quiet, his son to the Stone-Fall (the boy had gone to the Highland mines for the wages), and his faith in most things somewhere in between.

Now he walks the streets at dusk with a long lighting pole and a running commentary on doom. "Every lamp I light is one less dark," he says. "The dark doesn't mind. It has more." He is convinced the village is ending, has been convinced for thirty years, and has never missed a single evening's round.

He is the only villager who comes out once the Ashlands are open, because, he says, now that the worst has started he might as well watch it properly. When the land turns green he complains about the light. He is, secretly, delighted.

## In the Saga
- Lines along the lamp-posts at dusk (from the Ashlands on).
- He knows the Binders lately come over the walls for the Grasslands dead — "I've seen the embers at the edge of the light."

## Ties
- Oswin: avoids him at dusk.
- Ilsa: the only person who finds his gloom poetic, and says so.
- Benno: the one person whose cheer Fenwick cannot dent, which annoys him deeply.

# Ilsa the Lighthouse Keeper
key: ilsa
who: Ilsa the Lighthouse Keeper
meets: 2
age: 50
home: the lighthouse (after the end)
pick: Lonely and poetic
voice: Quiet, lyrical and a little lost; speaks in images of water and light, and listens more than she talks.

## Backstory
Ilsa came to the last village at fourteen, from a harbour town on the Ashland coast, sent away by her parents the summer before the Ash Night "to learn a trade". She learned lamp-keeping from the old lighthouse keeper on the point and never went home, because there was no home to go to. The lighthouse has guided no ships for twenty-eight years. She has lit it every night anyway.

She writes poems to the dark water — not love poems like Corr's, but long, quiet ones to the people she can't see. She keeps them in a sea-chest. She has watched the lake every night of her adult life and has seen things nobody believes: two little lights on the water one stormy dawn, and, on still nights, a reflection of something golden in the sky that isn't there when you look up.

After the end, ships come back. She lights the lighthouse the first night a sail appears, and has to sit down on the stair.

## In the Saga
- The lighthouse, after the end.
- Saw the basket's lights from the point the dawn of the storm (and never told anyone, because no one asked).

## Ties
- Fenwick: finds his gloom beautiful.
- Nell: the two of them watch the lake from either end of the harbour.
- Corr: the poems' subject. She knows. She has been waiting twenty years for him to bring one up the hill.

# Pip
key: pip
who: Pip
meets: 2
age: born after the end
home: the fountain on the Green (after the end)
pick: Endless why-questions

## Backstory
Pip is one of the first children born in Runeheim after the end, in the first spring when the road was open again. By the time you meet Pip, Pip has learned to walk, to talk and, above all, to ask why. Why is the sky blue? Why are there spires in the lake? Why does Master Oswin shout? Why do you have rune eyes? Why?

Pip has grown up on the stories of the twin who went and the twin who stayed, mostly as told by Oswin, which means Pip believes you once punched a fire giant into the sea. Pip wants to know why you didn't punch him into the sky.

## In the Saga

## Ties
- Wren: best friend and rival; Wren goes places, Pip asks why.

- Benno: the first honey cake ever eaten in the new age was Pip's. Pip asked why it was a fox.

# Wren

home: the market carts (after the end)
pick: The fearless explorer

## Backstory
Wren is one of the first children of the new age, born the same spring as Pip, and has never stood still in her life. She has climbed the Runestone, the windmill, and very nearly the lighthouse. She has a map she drew herself of "every road in Runeheim", which is mostly the roads around the market and one enormous arrow pointing west labelled ADVENTURES.

Wren wants to be you. Specifically, she wants to be the one who goes out. She has a wooden sword from Garrick, a cape from Pell's offcuts, and a list of monsters she intends to fight, which is long and entirely out of date, since the monsters faded at the end. She has not been told this. Nobody has the heart.

## In the Saga
- After the end, at the market carts.

## Ties
- Pip: best friend; Wren goes, Pip asks why.
- Garrick: made her wooden sword and pretends it was an accident.
